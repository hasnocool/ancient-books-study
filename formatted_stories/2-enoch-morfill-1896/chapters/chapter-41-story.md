## Story

Enoch sees the forefathers from the beginning, including Adam and Eve. He sighs and weeps as he reflects on the ruin caused by their wickedness. In his distress he calls blessed the person who was never born, or who, having been born, never sinned before the Lord and therefore never had to enter that place and bear its burden.

## Source notes and adaptation choices

- Narrative adaptation of Chapter 41, not a replacement for the transcription.
- Names, numbers, sequence, and unusual descriptions are retained; no external harmonization is attempted.
- The vision turns Enoch’s attention from the ordered cosmos to the consequences of human wrongdoing. His lament is an expression of grief at the fate of sinners, not a general instruction to reject life. The phrase about the burden of “this place” refers back to the place of suffering in the preceding vision.
