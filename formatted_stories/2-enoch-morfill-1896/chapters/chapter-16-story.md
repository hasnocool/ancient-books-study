## Story

The guides show Enoch the moon's course and revolutions. They point out twelve great gates stretching from west to east, through which the moon enters and leaves at its appointed times.

The text gives a sequence of periods for the moon's passage through the twelve gates: thirty-one days at the first, second, fifth, sixth, eighth, ninth, and eleventh; thirty days at the third, fourth, seventh, and tenth; and twenty-eight days at the twelfth.

The moon travels through the western gates in correspondence with the eastern gates and completes its year. The text then compares the solar and lunar counts. It gives the solar year as 365 days and a quarter, and the lunar year as 354 days. It also says that the lunar year consists of twelve months of twenty-nine days, with eleven days remaining as lunar epacts in the solar year.

The account adds that the quarter day is neglected for three years and completed in the fourth. It describes changes to the seasons and the addition and subtraction of months, then says that the moon returns through the western gates toward the east, moving through the heavenly circles more swiftly than the winds. Spirits, creatures, and six-winged angels fly there as well.

The final verse states that seven months are computed to the moon's circle during a revolution of nineteen years.
