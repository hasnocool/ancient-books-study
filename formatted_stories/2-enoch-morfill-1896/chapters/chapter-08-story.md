## Story

The messengers take Enoch from the second heaven to the third. They place him in a garden whose beauty, the text says, has no earthly comparison.

He sees trees of beautiful colours, bearing ripe and fragrant fruit, and many kinds of food growing with a delightful scent. At the centre stands the tree of life, the place where God rests when he comes into Paradise. Its excellence and fragrance are beyond description. It is more beautiful than anything created, appearing like gold and crimson, transparent as fire, and spreading over everything.

Four streams flow from the tree's root. They carry honey, milk, oil, and wine, and divide toward the four directions. Their courses are gentle. They descend toward the Paradise of Eden, between corruptibility and incorruptibility, then proceed across the earth and circle like the other elements.

Enoch also sees an olive tree that continually yields oil. No tree in the garden is without fruit, and every tree is blessed.

Three hundred glorious angels keep the garden. Their voices and blessed singing never cease as they serve the Lord each day. Enoch marvels at the blessedness of the place.
