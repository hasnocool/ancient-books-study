## Story

The sermon begins by declaring God the glory and source of all things. God is described through a series of encompassing terms: Godhead, Nature, Mind, Matter, Wisdom, Energy, Necessity, End, and the power of making things new again. Before the ordered world appears, boundless Darkness, Water, and an intelligent subtle Breath exist in Chaos through God's power. Holy Light then arises. Elements gather beneath Dry Space from Moist Essence, and the gods separate things out from fertile Nature.

The account next describes how the unformed things are assigned their places. The lighter things are placed above, while the heavier ones receive foundations below the moist region of Dry Space. Fire bounds the universal things, and Breath holds them up. Heaven appears in seven circles. Its gods become visible as stars with their signs, while Nature's members are brought into articulation together with the gods within her. The circumference of Heaven turns in a cycle, carried by the Breath of God.

Each god then brings forth what has been appointed through that god's own power. The resulting living world includes four-footed animals, creeping things, creatures of the water, winged beings, seed-bearing plants, grasses, and flowering shoots. Each carries within itself the seed of renewed becoming.

The sermon explains that human beings are brought forth for several purposes: to know God's works, to bear witness to Nature's energy, and to exercise lordship over what is beneath Heaven. Humanity is to increase and multiply. Each soul takes on flesh through the revolution of the cyclic gods, so that it may observe the wonders of Heaven, the revolutions of the heavenly gods, and the works of God and Nature. Through these observations, human beings may recognize divine power, understand the fates that follow good and evil deeds, and learn the skill of the good arts.

Human life, growth in wisdom, and death then occur according to the fate appointed by the cycles of the gods. Human handiworks leave great memorials on Earth, although only dim traces remain when cycles are renewed. Every ensouled bodily birth, every fruit produced from seed, and every human handiwork decays; yet each must renew itself through the renewal of the gods and the turning wheel of Nature.

The sermon closes by describing Godhead as Nature's ever-renewing power over the cosmic mixture. Nature herself, in turn, is established together with that Godhead. The world is therefore presented as a recurring process of ordered generation, decay, and renewal.

## Source notes and adaptation choices

- This is a prose summary of the four numbered sections, not a new translation.
- The progression from divine source, primordial conditions, cosmic ordering, generation of living things, human purpose, and renewal is retained.
- “Mind,” “Nature,” “Matter,” “Wisdom,” “Breath,” “Godhead,” and “Cyclic Gods” follow Mead's English rendering.
- The text's claim that humanity has lordship over what is beneath Heaven is reported as a claim within the sermon, not endorsed as a modern ethical position.
- The sermon links human life and fate to the cycles of heavenly gods; the adaptation does not convert this into a modern astronomical or astrological system.
- The phrase “Dry Space” includes a translator's gloss identifying the underlying word literally with “sand.” The phrase is retained rather than silently replaced.
- Section 4 begins with the bracketed “[Thus]”; this editorial bracket is preserved as part of the source transcription.
- No missing material, dialogue, or additional scenes have been invented.
