# Of Asclepius to the King — Story Adaptation

> **Source:** G. R. S. Mead, *Thrice-Greatest Hermes*, Volume II (1906), supplementary fragment.
> **Status:** Provisional adaptation of the formatted transcription; not facsimile-collated or independently human-reviewed.

Asclepius asks the King to consider whether something without a body can nevertheless appear through a body. The King agrees when Asclepius points to images seen in mirrors: their appearances are visible, though the reflections themselves have no material body in the ordinary sense.

Asclepius extends the question to forms. Forms seem bodiless, yet appear in bodies, whether those bodies are ensouled or not. He then describes a reciprocal relation: the Sensible may be reflected in the Intelligible, and the Intelligible in the Sensible. The King is urged to worship images because their forms, too, derive from the Intelligible World.

The King rises and ends the conversation for the evening, proposing that the sacred discussion resume the next day.
