## Story

Enoch presents a blessing for the righteous who will be alive during a time of great trouble, when wickedness is to be removed. He explains that God opened his eyes to a vision shown by the angels. The vision was not intended only for his own generation, but for people in distant generations to come.

Enoch then describes the coming of the Holy and Great One from his dwelling. The divine appearance is accompanied by hosts and overwhelming power. The watchers tremble, fear spreads across the earth, mountains shake and sink, and the earth itself is torn apart. The vision depicts a judgment extending over the whole world.

Yet the message is not only one of destruction. The righteous and chosen are promised peace, protection, grace, blessing, and the light of God. Enoch closes by describing the Holy One coming with vast numbers of holy ones to judge the ungodly and expose the wrongs committed against Him.

## Source notes and adaptation choices

- The adaptation keeps the chapter’s movement from blessing, to vision, to cosmic judgment, and finally to protection for the righteous.
- “Ten thousands” is retained as the source’s expression of a very great host; no exact headcount is inferred.
- The chapter’s future-oriented language is preserved without assigning it a modern date or identifying a particular historical event.
