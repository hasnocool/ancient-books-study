## Story

Enoch again looks at the natural world. Trees put out green leaves and bear fruit, while the seas and rivers continue to perform their work. The recurring order of creation is presented as evidence of the enduring Creator, whose works remain under His command from year to year.

Against this picture of steadiness, Enoch addresses people who have not remained faithful. He says they have failed to keep the Lord’s law and have spoken arrogantly and abusively against divine greatness. He warns that this conduct will bring disgrace, loss, and the absence of mercy. The wicked will become an enduring example of condemnation among the righteous.

The future of the chosen is described very differently. They are promised light, joy, peace, and an inheritance of the earth. Wisdom will be given to them. They will live without returning to sin through carelessness or pride, and humility will characterize the wise. Their lives will not be cut short by the punishments described for the wicked; instead, they will reach their full span in peace and lasting happiness.

## Source notes and adaptation choices

- The adaptation preserves the chapter’s contrast between creation’s regularity, human unfaithfulness, and the different outcomes described for the wicked and the chosen.
- “Inheritance of the earth” is retained as the source’s promise; the passage does not specify a political territory or modern geographic claim.
- The source’s language about the chosen no longer sinning is reported as the vision’s promise, not generalized into a claim about present-day people.
