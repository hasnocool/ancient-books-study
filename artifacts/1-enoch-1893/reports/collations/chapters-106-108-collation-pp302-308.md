# Chapters CVI–CVIII text-layer and apparatus collation — printed pp.302–308

**Witness:** R. H. Charles, *The Book of Enoch* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1893), Cornell University Library copy, Internet Archive identifier `cu31924014590545`.

**Method and boundary:** Sequential collation of the Cornell searchable OCR/text layer and Charles's critical/exegetical notes for printed pp.302–308, compared with the repository's formatted Book 6 (Chapters 106–108). OCR is a finding aid; Ethiopic characters and manuscript sigla are frequently corrupted. This is not page-image inspection and is not a complete diplomatic transcription.

## 1. Literary and bibliographic status

The 1893 volume's contents identify Section V as XCI–CIV, but the translation pagination continues through pp.265–308. After CV.1–2 on p.301, the printed translation includes CVI–CVIII on pp.302–308:

- **CVI–CVII:** Charles explicitly identifies these chapters as another fragment of a Noah Apocalypse. They are present in the 1893 printed translation; their distinct literary status is not a reason to omit them from the 1893 witness.
- **CVIII:** Charles calls this final chapter an independent addition. He says its writer knew Sections I–XXXVI and XCI–CIV, though his acquaintance with I–XXXVI was inaccurate.
- **Appendix E:** The 1893 volume separately prints a new Latin fragment of CVI.1–18 with introduction and notes on pp.372–375. This is a distinct Latin witness, not a substitute for the Ethiopic translation of CVI in pp.302–304.

## 3. Translation and apparatus collation

### Chapter CVI (106)

- **CVI.1–2:** The chapter opens with Methuselah arranging a wife for Lamech; Noah is born with extraordinary appearance. The printed text reads “white as snow and red as a blooming rose,” with hair white as wool. The apparatus at v.2 records a witness variant “His colour.”
- **CVI.3–4:** Noah opens his eyes, illuminates the house, and speaks with the Lord of righteousness; Charles's note says the phrase “Lord of heaven” at v.11 is unique in Enoch, while the language of speaking/conversing with the Lord is treated separately.
- **CVI.5–7:** Lamech fears the child is angelic and asks Methuselah to consult Enoch. Preserve the distinction between Lamech's suspicion and Enoch's eventual explanation.
- **CVI.8–10:** Methuselah travels to Enoch at the ends of the earth. At v.9, G/G1/M support “cause of anxiety”; other manuscripts have “matter.” At v.10, the witnesses differ in the form of the address “Hear me, my father”; do not reconstruct the Ethiopic wording from OCR glyphs.
- **CVI.11–12:** The newborn blesses the Lord of heaven; Lamech again seeks an explanation from Enoch. The apparatus records variants around the phrase “his father, Lamech” at v.12.
- **CVI.13:** Enoch says the Lord will do a new thing and explains that, in Jared's generation, some from the heights of heaven transgressed. G omits a phrase in “this I have already seen”; witnesses differ in the clause “some from the heights of heaven.” Charles notes an easy conjectural emendation “some of the angels of heaven,” but this is not the printed reading and must remain a conjecture.
- **CVI.14:** The Watchers' sin is described as transgressing the law and uniting with women. Charles explains “the law” as the law appointed to them as spiritual beings; this is commentary, not an added translation gloss.
- **CVI.15:** The printed translation predicts a deluge and great destruction “for one year.” G reads “on the earth”; other manuscripts have “on the whole earth.”
- **CVI.16:** The printed text says the child and his three sons will be left/saved. Charles explicitly omits words found in Dillmann after “all mankind that are on the earth shall die” because G does not support them: “he and his children will be saved. They will beget on earth.” Do not reinsert this longer wording into the Charles translation.
- **CVI.17:** The printed verse says the giants are not according to spirit but according to flesh. Charles states that the first half of this verse, ending “on the earth,” has been transposed from its proper position after v.14; in that earlier position it should read “Giants, who are not according…” Preserve the printed sequence while recording the proposed relocation. This is a textual-order correction, not permission to renumber silently.
- **CVI.18:** Enoch tells Methuselah to assure Lamech that the child is his son and to name him Noah. The apparatus records the derivation of the name Noah from the root associated with rest/comfort.
- **CVI.19:** The chapter closes with further unrighteousness and Enoch's knowledge of the mysteries recorded in the heavenly tables. Charles allows that “mysteries of the holy ones” may mean angelic secrets or secrets concerning the future righteous.

### Chapter CVII (107)

- **CVII.1:** Enoch sees a record that generations will transgress until a generation of righteousness arises. G/G1's reading is rendered in the apparatus as “that a generation of righteousness will arise”; the printed English retains “till a generation of righteousness arises.”
- **CVII.2–3:** Enoch sends Methuselah to tell Lamech the child is truly his son; Methuselah returns, repeats the revelation, and names the child Noah. The apparatus says G reads “again” at the opening of v.3; M supports “returned and showed (them) to him,” while G omits part of that phrase. Other witnesses and Dillmann have “returned, after having seen him.”
- **Commentary:** Charles describes CVII.1 as the renewed growth of sin after the Flood and the eventual advent of the Messianic kingdom. The short chapter is tied to the Noah fragment, not to the independent CVIII addition.

### Chapter CVIII (108)

- **CVIII.1:** Introduces “another book” written by Enoch for Methuselah and later law-keepers. Charles calls the chapter an independent addition.
- **CVIII.2:** The faithful are told to wait until evil-doers and transgressors are ended. G/Q and other witnesses vary significantly in the clause rendered “Ye who have done good will wait for those days”; preserve the printed English and record variants rather than harmonizing them.
- **CVIII.3:** Sinners' names are blotted out of the book of life and the books of the holy ones. G/G1 support “out of the book of life and”; M has “out of the book”; other manuscripts omit the additional phrase. The OCR apparatus is damaged after this point; do not present Ethiopic characters as verified.
- **CVIII.4–6:** The passage describes a place of fire and punishment for sinners, blasphemers, and those who pervert God's revelation through the prophets. Charles argues that the author has confused Gehenna with the separate place of punishment for the disobedient stars described in Chapters XVIII and XXI.
- **CVIII.7–10:** The humble and those who afflict their bodies are contrasted with the wicked. The righteous love God, reject gold and silver and worldly goods, endure torture and abuse, and receive recompense. At v.9 the printed “regarded their bodies as a breath that passeth away” is glossed by Charles as ascetic contempt for the body.
- **CVIII.11–15:** God summons spirits belonging to the generation of light; the faithful are brought forth in shining light and seated on thrones, while those remaining in darkness are cast into darkness. Variants include “sought not honour” (G) versus alternatives in FHILNO/Dillmann, and “clad in shining light” (G) versus a phrase translated by Dillmann as “bring forth into a brightly shining light.” Charles prefers “clad in shining light” because the next verse says they will be resplendent. At v.13 G has a different construction for “in the habitation of upright paths”; at v.14 G varies in “will be cast”; at v.15 G differs in “as they shine.”

## 4. Charles's commentary, separately attributed

- **CVI–CVII as Noah fragment:** Charles says this fragment uses the Samaritan reckoning. On that chronology Enoch is still alive when Noah is born, as presupposed by Chapters LXV–LXVI; Hebrew and LXX chronologies would place Noah's birth after Enoch's translation.
- **CVI.3–6:** Charles sees the chapter as borrowing language from the earlier Watchers narrative, particularly the angelic appearance of Noah and the supernatural speech at birth.
- **CVI.13–15:** He connects the transgression with the Watchers and the law appointed to spiritual beings. The one-year Flood is compared with Genesis.
- **CVI.17:** Charles identifies a transposition in the verse and explicitly proposes moving its first half to follow v.14. The repository should preserve the printed verse order and annotate this editorial reconstruction.
- **CVI.18–19:** Charles discusses the etymology of Noah and the “heavenly tables.”
- **CVII:** Charles summarizes the chapter as describing the post-Flood growth of sin and the eventual rise of a righteous generation.
- **CVIII:** Charles says this final chapter is an independent addition. He considers its author Essene in tone, noting asceticism and disdain for gold, silver, and earthly food. He argues the writer's acquaintance with I–XXXVI is inaccurate. He sees CVIII.3–6 as conflating Gehenna with the prison of the disobedient stars, and CVIII.11–14 as emphasizing dualism between light and darkness and the blessed immortality of the soul, apparently not the body.

## 5. Repository comparison and corrections

This pass is a targeted OCR/apparatus comparison. It does not authorize wholesale replacement of the repository text. Key editorial points to carry forward:

1. Preserve the printed sequence of CVI.17 while documenting Charles's proposed transposition of its first half after v.14.
2. Preserve Charles's omission at CVI.16 of the extra Dillmann wording unsupported by G.
3. Keep CVI–CVII labelled as the Noachian fragment and CVIII as the independent final addition; do not collapse their literary distinctions.
4. Distinguish the translated Ethiopic text of CVI in pp.302–304 from the separate Latin fragment of CVI.1–18 in Appendix E, pp.372–375.
