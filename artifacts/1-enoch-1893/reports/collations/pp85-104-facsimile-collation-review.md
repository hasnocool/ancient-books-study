## Confirmed corrections to the OCR/alignment pilots

1. **XV.12, p.85:** withdraw the pilot's alleged omission of “and against the women.” The facsimile plainly contains those words.
2. **XVI, p.86:** the Greek fragment is explicitly identified as absent from the Ethiopic version. It must remain classified as a separate witness, not missing body text.
3. **XVII–XIX, pp.87–91:** the opening bracket is printed at XVII.1 on p.87 and the closing bracket after XIX.3 on p.91. Record the complete bracket span, not a local OCR bracket artifact.
4. **XX.7, p.92:** preserve “the serpents and the Cherubin” as body text. “The serpents may be Seraphim” is Charles's apparatus explanation.
5. **XXV.1, p.98:** the printed reading is “what dost thou ask”; the derivative's “what do you ask” is modernization, not a facsimile reading.
6. **XXXI.3, p.102:** the printed reading explicitly says “the taste of that fruit” and includes a parenthetical literal gloss. The derivative's “One of that fruit” is not an equivalent diplomatic transcription.
7. **XXXV/XXXVI, p.104:** XXXV is the westward unit and XXXVI.1 is the southward unit. The pilot's alleged single-locus direction variant is withdrawn.

## Repeated pattern requiring systematic pass

Across pp.87–104, Charles's printed narrative frequently begins clauses or verses with “And” where the derivative omits the conjunction or restructures the sentence. These are not automatically substantive variants, but they are part of a diplomatic comparison and must be logged consistently. Other recurring categories are modernized second-person forms (“thou/thee” → “you”), parenthetical clarifications, punctuation, and verse-opening syntax.
