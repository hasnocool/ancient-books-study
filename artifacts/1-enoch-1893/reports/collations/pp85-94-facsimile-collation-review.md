## Confirmed witness readings and editorial cautions

1. **XXI.7 (printed p.92):** Cornell body text reads “Paradise and the serpents and the Cherubin.” Charles's apparatus separately proposes that the serpents may be Seraphim. The body reading and explanatory hypothesis are distinct textual layers.
2. **Interpolation boundary (pp.87–91):** The opening square bracket is printed at XVII.1 on p.87; the closing square bracket follows XIX.3 on p.91. The bracketed unit therefore spans XVII.1–XIX.3 in this witness.
3. **Greek-only fragment (p.86):** The page itself states that the fragment is absent from the Ethiopic version. It is not evidence of an omitted Ethiopic narrative paragraph.
4. **p.91 OCR provenance:** Cornell's OCR is absent. The parallel Knox/Toronto extraction is a supplementary witness only; the visible Cornell JPEG is the primary evidence for this page.
5. **XXI.5 (p.93):** The printed wording is “Wherefore dost thou ask, and why dost thou enquire and art curious?” Later-witness alternatives are discussed in the apparatus. The modernized derivative wording is not a diplomatic rendering of the Cornell/1893 line.
6. **Angel name (p.94):** The body text reads “Rufael.” Keep this spelling as the witness reading; do not harmonize it with alternate transliterations without an explicit editorial policy.
