# 1 Enoch 2 — Verse-by-Verse Visual Interpretation Prompts

> **Status:** provisional prompt set  
> **Passage:** 1 Enoch 2:1–3  
> **Text basis:** Repository Chapter 2 transcription; not yet verified word-for-word against the 1893 facsimile  
> **Purpose:** Five separate visual interpretations for each verse  
> **Created:** 2026-11-26

## How to use these prompts

Generate each prompt as a separate image. Do not combine the five lenses into one composite. The ancient-horizon image is the historical baseline. Astronomic/astronautic and human-origin/seeding images are speculative alternatives and must be captioned as such.

Use historically cautious ancient material culture and a serious naturalistic illustration style. Avoid modern astronomical diagrams, telescopes, satellites, spacecraft, pseudo-ancient writing, captions, labels, watermarks, and readable lettering unless a prompt explicitly requests a speculative modern analogy. Do not make the images look like documentary evidence.

---

## 2:1 — The heavenly lights keep their appointed courses

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create a historically grounded scene of an ancient sky observer watching celestial lights rise and set over a familiar landscape. Show the sun near the horizon and stars or other visible lights in the night sky, with an attentive observer and perhaps a simple calendar-keeping context. Use period-appropriate clothing and architecture, no modern telescope or orbital diagram. Convey the ancient sense that the heavens follow an appointed and dependable order.

### Literal/material prompt
Depict the observable rising and setting of heavenly lights across the horizon over time. Use a naturalistic sky and terrestrial viewpoint. The image should show regular celestial appearances without adding modern scientific labels, mathematically exact orbit paths, planets not identified by the verse, or instruments the text does not mention.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a clearly speculative alternate visualization of a distant or elevated observer studying recurring celestial movements. If an unfamiliar vantage point or non-human observer is included, make it visually ambiguous and explicitly conjectural; do not show a recognizable spacecraft, satellite, cockpit, or modern orbital display. The image explores a modern hypothesis, not the verse's established meaning.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Show a speculative scene of human sky-watchers passing knowledge of celestial cycles to younger generations. Include careful observation, seasonal record-keeping, and shared teaching in a plausible ancient setting. Do not portray extraterrestrial teachers or advanced technology; the visual focus is human transmission of astronomical knowledge.

### Human / social prompt
Depict a community organizing its calendar and shared activities through observation of the sky. Show elders teaching younger people to recognize recurring celestial patterns, with daily life and seasonal preparation in the background. Emphasize continuity, discipline, and shared knowledge.

## 2:2 — The earth displays the consistency of divine work

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create a wide ancient landscape showing the inhabited earth as people experienced it: fields, paths, water, trees, animals, homes, and distant hills. Include a person carefully observing the land. The scene should convey the idea that ordinary earthly processes display a dependable created order, using historically plausible details rather than modern environmental science imagery.

### Literal/material prompt
Render a grounded terrestrial panorama of observable natural processes and recurring life: plants growing, water moving, animals feeding, and people working the land. Keep the viewpoint at human scale. Do not show the earth as a globe from space or add processes that the verse does not specify.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a clearly speculative high-altitude view of a broad inhabited landscape, as a modern artistic analogy for observing the earth as a whole. Do not imply that the ancient author literally viewed Earth from orbit. Avoid spacecraft, satellites, screens, and technical instruments; keep the image labelled externally as a speculative visualization.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Depict a speculative scene of human communities sharing knowledge about land, water, cultivation, and settlement. Show practical teaching between generations and the gradual shaping of a lived environment. Do not present migration, population seeding, or advanced intervention as facts stated by the verse.

### Human / social prompt
Show the interdependence of a community and its environment: farmers, families, travellers, animals, and neighbours sharing the same landscape. The image should communicate humility, stewardship, and the need to pay attention to the world people inhabit.

## 2:3 — Summer, winter, water, clouds, dew, and rain

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create a paired seasonal landscape in an ancient setting, showing the same region in summer and winter. Include clouds, rain, dew on plants at dawn, and water sustaining the land. Make the seasonal differences regionally plausible rather than assuming identical climates everywhere. The scene should reflect an ancient observer's close attention to weather and agricultural conditions.

### Literal/material prompt
Depict the concrete weather and moisture phenomena named in the verse: clouds, dew, rain, water, and seasonal change. Use a naturalistic landscape with water on vegetation and rainfall over the ground. Do not turn “the whole earth is full of water” into a universal flood; preserve the phrase's ambiguity.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a clearly speculative high-altitude view of cloud systems and seasonal weather over a broad land area, presented as a modern visualization rather than an ancient eyewitness reconstruction. Do not add weather-control devices, satellites, spacecraft, or claims that the verse describes orbital observation.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Show a speculative ancient community adapting to seasonal water patterns: collecting rain, tending fields, storing water, caring for livestock, and teaching practical knowledge to younger people. Do not depict advanced climate engineering or claim that the verse records a deliberate seeding operation.

### Human / social prompt
Depict how seasonal water shapes shared life: people preparing fields, gathering water, sheltering from rain, tending animals, and helping neighbours through changing weather. Emphasize cooperation, dependence on the environment, and the value of shared practical knowledge.
