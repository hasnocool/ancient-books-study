## How to use these prompts

Generate each prompt as a separate image. Do not combine the five lenses into one composite. The ancient-horizon image is the historical baseline. Astronomic/astronautic and human-origin/seeding images are speculative alternatives and must be captioned as such.

Use a naturalistic, historically cautious visual style. Do not invent the tree species, geographic location, ritual context, or meaning of the number fourteen. Avoid readable text, captions, labels, pseudo-script, watermarks, modern botanical charts, and documentary-evidence styling. Keep the two-to-three-year foliage detail as a textual claim, not a scientifically verified measurement.

## 3:1 — Most trees shed their leaves; fourteen retain theirs

### Ancient-horizon prompt

Create a historically grounded scene of an ancient observer standing in a grove during a seasonal transition. Most trees appear withered and have shed their leaves, while a distinct group of fourteen trees retains its foliage. Suggest that new growth is beginning among the retained old leaves. Use a plausible ancient landscape and simple clothing; do not assign a species, precise region, symbolic meaning, or ritual to the trees. Convey careful observation of variation in the natural world.

### Literal/material prompt

Create a naturalistic botanical landscape showing the contrast stated in 1 Enoch 3:1: trees that appear withered and have shed their leaves, except for fourteen trees retaining old foliage as new foliage comes. The image should communicate the unusual two-to-three-year retention as overlapping old and new leaves, without diagrams, labels, species identification, or invented mechanisms. Do not depict the fourteen as glowing, supernatural, or physically engineered.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt

Create a clearly speculative alternate visualization of the grove viewed from an unfamiliar elevated vantage point. The main visual focus remains the contrast between the ordinary leaf-shedding trees and the fourteen foliage-retaining trees. If an unusual observer or technological viewpoint is suggested, keep it abstract and visibly conjectural; do not add spacecraft, alien beings, scanning beams, or engineered vegetation. This is a modern hypothesis image, not the verse's established meaning.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt

Create a clearly speculative scene of ancient people comparing tree varieties and passing botanical knowledge between generations in a grove. Include the contrast between leaf-shedding trees and fourteen trees retaining foliage, but do not claim that the fourteen were imported, selectively engineered, genetically modified, or deliberately seeded. The image explores a possible human history of plant observation and knowledge transmission, not an event stated in the verse.

### Human / social prompt

Show a small ancient community learning from the seasonal behaviour of trees. An experienced observer points out the difference between the leaf-shedding trees and the fourteen that retain foliage, while younger members watch and learn. Use historically cautious clothing and surroundings. Emphasize attentiveness, shared environmental knowledge, and intergenerational learning; do not invent a formal ceremony or a named culture.
