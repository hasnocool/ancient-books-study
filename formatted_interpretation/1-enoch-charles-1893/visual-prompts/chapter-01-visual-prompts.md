# 1 Enoch 1 — Verse-by-Verse Visual Interpretation Prompts

> **Status:** provisional prompt set  
> **Passage:** 1 Enoch 1:1–9  
> **Text basis:** Repository Chapter 1 transcription; not yet verified word-for-word against the 1893 facsimile  
> **Purpose:** Five distinct visual interpretations for every verse, matching the five interpretive lenses in the written study  
> **Created:** 2026-11-26

## How to use these prompts

Each prompt is an independent image-generation brief. Generate one image per prompt; do not combine the five lenses into a single composite. The **ancient-horizon** prompt is the historically grounded baseline. The **astronomic / astronautic** and **human-origin / seeding** prompts are deliberately speculative alternate readings, not claims established by the text.

Unless a prompt specifies otherwise, use a serious historical-illustration aesthetic: researched ancient Near Eastern visual culture, restrained naturalistic lighting, tactile materials, coherent anatomy, cinematic but not fantastical composition, and no modern objects. Avoid pseudo-ancient writing, captions, labels, borders, watermarks, or legible text in the image. Do not depict later medieval or modern religious iconography as though it were contemporary with the text.

Where the wording leaves details unspecified, preserve that uncertainty. Do not add spacecraft, instruments, uniforms, biological features, weapons, or mechanisms merely to make a speculative interpretation more dramatic. The image is an interpretation of the verse, not evidence for it.

## 1:1 — Blessing for the elect and righteous in a time of distress

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create a historically grounded scene of a small ancient community gathered to hear an elder pronounce a solemn blessing during a period of danger. Show ordinary families, elders, and listeners in a modest ancient Near Eastern settlement, with signs of uncertainty beyond the community but no graphic violence. The focus is communal endurance, moral identity, and hope for the righteous. No supernatural spectacle is required.

### Literal/material prompt
Visualize the verse as a concrete future crisis: a community identified as righteous remains together while the surrounding settlement faces severe disruption and the wicked are being removed from the community's world. Keep the mechanism of removal unresolved—do not invent soldiers, machines, transport, or a specific catastrophe. Make the contrast visible without claiming more than the verse states.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create an explicitly speculative alternate visualization in which a threatened human community looks toward an immense, unfamiliar event in the sky while a selected group appears to be spared. Keep the source and mechanism ambiguous; do not show a recognizable spacecraft or identifiable extraterrestrial beings. The composition should communicate the modern hypothesis of external intervention while making clear visually that the text itself gives no such technical details.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative historical scene of a community preserving a chosen group of people through a period of collapse, as though a lineage or founding population were being protected for the future. Show human elders, families, and the transfer of practical knowledge between generations. Do not depict genetic engineering, laboratories, or biological selection; the verse speaks of the elect and righteous, not genetics.

### Human / social prompt
Depict the social experience of living through a crisis: mutual care, fear, unequal power, moral choices, and the hope that injustice will not have the final word. Center the faces and relationships of ordinary people rather than a cosmic spectacle. Avoid portraying the “wicked” as an ethnic or physically distinct group.

## 1:2 — Enoch receives a vision for distant generations

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Show an ancient seer in a quiet, simple setting experiencing a revelatory vision, with a restrained heavenly scene suggested above or beyond him. A small number of messenger figures may appear in a culturally appropriate ancient visual idiom, but avoid later angel wings, halos, or medieval robes. Emphasize listening, insight, and the preservation of a message for people not yet born.

### Literal/material prompt
Visualize a person who sees and hears a real encounter with beings understood by him as heavenly messengers. Keep the scene spatially concrete but deliberately indeterminate: the beings' bodies, the location, and the means of communication are not described. Do not add a device, book, or technology unless shown only as an unobtrusive human means of preserving the message.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a clearly speculative alternate scene in which an ancient observer encounters unfamiliar non-human visitors in an elevated or sky-associated setting and receives information intended for later humanity. Avoid recognizable modern spacecraft, screens, spacesuits, or stereotyped “grey alien” designs. Make the encounter visually ambiguous between a heavenly vision and an unfamiliar physical meeting.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Depict a speculative knowledge-transfer scene: an older human teacher or visiting group passes information to a witness who will preserve it for descendants. Use plausible ancient clothing and objects only. Do not portray the visitors as genetically engineered or claim that the verse describes a breeding program; the visual idea is transmission across generations.

### Human / social prompt
Show the responsibility of a witness who receives knowledge on behalf of people in the distant future. Place the seer in the foreground and suggest generations yet to come through subtle family and community silhouettes in the distance. The emotional center is stewardship, memory, and the burden of transmitting a warning accurately.

## 1:3 — The Holy and Great One comes forth from His dwelling

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Visualize a majestic divine-royal procession emerging from a sacred heavenly dwelling, using the visual grammar of ancient kingship: a threshold, attendants, ordered movement, and a sense of supreme authority. Keep the divine figure symbolic or partially veiled rather than imposing a later doctrinal portrait. The scene should feel like a sovereign's public arrival.

### Literal/material prompt
Depict the concrete action stated: a powerful figure leaves an abode and comes forth. Show a distinct threshold and movement from one place into another, but do not specify the abode's architecture, the figure's anatomy, or a vehicle. Keep the unknowns visibly unresolved.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate visualization of an immense presence emerging from an elevated, enclosed structure in the sky and appearing over the world below. The structure may suggest an unfamiliar aerial or orbital habitat only in abstract silhouette; do not add engines, lights, panels, or spacecraft details absent from the verse. The image must read as a hypothesis, not a reconstruction.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Show a speculative culture-bringer or powerful founder emerging from a protected residence to enter the human world. Use an ancient human setting and a small retinue, with no supernatural certainty. Do not portray this as proven history or add claims of non-human ancestry.

### Human / social prompt
Depict the public arrival of authority: a community watches as a ruler leaves seclusion and becomes answerable for action. Explore the tension between awe, hope, and fear when power enters public view. Avoid making the image simply a portrait of domination; suggest the expectation of judgment and responsibility.

## 1:4 — Arrival at Mount Sinai with His hosts

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create a historically sensitive theophany at a rugged Sinai-like mountain: a commanding heavenly presence, a vast ordered host, and a landscape understood as a meeting place between heaven and earth. Use ancient Near Eastern visual references without claiming certainty about the exact historical appearance of Sinai. Avoid modern religious iconography.

### Literal/material prompt
Show a figure arriving at a named mountain accompanied by a large company. Render the mountain, the group, and the act of arrival as physically present, but leave the means of descent and the nature of the host unspecified. Do not add aircraft, armour styles, or weapons not required by the wording.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate scene in which a large formation of unfamiliar visitors appears above a mountain and descends toward it. Keep the formation indistinct and the means of travel unresolved; no recognizable spacecraft, thrusters, or modern flight technology. The image should invite a question about aerial arrival without pretending the text identifies technology.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Depict a speculative migration party arriving at a mountain stronghold: a leader and a large group approach a landscape that could become a place of settlement or cultural influence. Show human-scale movement and material burdens, but do not imply that the verse explicitly describes colonization or population seeding.

### Human / social prompt
Show how a powerful procession changes the emotional and political life of a community gathered near a sacred mountain. Emphasize scale, collective attention, leadership, fear, and the social meaning of public authority. Include ordinary people as witnesses, not merely distant decoration.

## 1:5 — The earth trembles and the Watchers are shaken

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Depict an ancient cosmic theophany in which the earth quakes, mountains tremble, and heavenly attendants appear unsettled before a supreme presence. Use the visual language of ancient prophetic poetry: storm, dust, unstable ground, and awe. Do not turn the scene into a modern disaster film.

### Literal/material prompt
Visualize a powerful physical disturbance affecting the ground and surrounding landscape, while a group of heavenly beings is shown trembling or withdrawing in fear. Keep the cause unspecified. Do not add explosions, weapons, or a measurable energy source.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate visualization of a large atmospheric or celestial event producing visible disturbance over the landscape, with unfamiliar observers or visitors reacting to it. Do not identify a specific astronomical object or technology; the verse does not provide one. Avoid laser effects and cinematic energy beams.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Show a speculative scene in which a human group witnessing a major environmental disturbance loses confidence in its leaders or guardians. The “Watchers” may be represented as powerful human overseers only as an explicitly interpretive choice, not as a translation. Do not depict this as established history.

### Human / social prompt
Depict the collapse of confidence in powerful authorities: ordinary people and once-confident guardians alike are unsettled by an event beyond their control. Focus on vulnerability, fear, and the reversal of status when established powers are shaken.

## 1:6 — Mountains shake and melt like wax

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create a vivid ancient poetic landscape: towering mountains tremble, high hills sink, and the image of wax before flame communicates overwhelming divine power. Use a naturalistic ancient landscape and symbolic visual exaggeration consistent with prophetic art. Do not claim the scene is a geological diagram.

### Literal/material prompt
Render severe landscape upheaval as concretely as possible: shaking slopes, collapsing ridges, and rock surfaces altered by intense heat. Keep the wax comparison recognizable as a simile rather than literally turning mountains into candle wax. Do not invent the cause.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate visualization of a catastrophic high-energy event affecting a mountainous region, with heat, dust, and terrain failure. The source may be suggested as an unidentified phenomenon in the sky but must remain visually indeterminate. No impact crater, beam weapon, spacecraft, or precise mechanism unless clearly treated as imaginative conjecture.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Depict a speculative human community forced to abandon a mountain homeland after a severe environmental disaster. Show people navigating a changed landscape and carrying essential belongings. Do not suggest that an advanced civilization deliberately terraformed the region; that is not stated in the verse.

### Human / social prompt
Show the human scale of a seemingly immovable world coming apart: families leaving familiar terrain, elders looking back at a collapsing landmark, and communities facing the loss of security. The image should communicate humility and impermanence rather than spectacle alone.

## 1:7 — The earth is rent and all face judgment

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Visualize an ancient apocalyptic image of the inhabited earth torn open, with humanity gathered under a universal tribunal. Use a symbolic but culturally grounded ancient world-picture: land, horizon, and a seat or sign of judgment. Avoid modern courtroom furniture and avoid depicting the righteous as exempt from appearing before judgment.

### Literal/material prompt
Show a physically fractured landscape and a universal reckoning in which all inhabitants are brought before judgment. The verse does not specify the exact geography, mechanism of destruction, or form of tribunal, so leave these details open rather than inventing them.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate image of a planet-scale crisis viewed from a distant vantage, with the inhabited world visibly disrupted and human survival uncertain. Do not show a specific asteroid, weapon, orbital platform, or alien fleet as though identified by the text. Keep the visual cause unresolved.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Depict a speculative civilization-ending crisis in which an entire population faces accountability and the possibility of historical rupture. Do not show a replacement population arriving or a repopulation operation; the verse describes judgment and destruction, not a new seeding event.

### Human / social prompt
Show a society in which rank, wealth, and influence no longer protect anyone from accountability. Bring people of different social positions into one shared scene of judgment. Avoid assigning guilt by ethnicity, disability, clothing, or physical appearance; moral accountability is the subject.

## 1:8 — Peace, protection, blessing, and divine light

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Depict a restored community living in security after distress: people gathered in peace, with warm light suggesting divine favour and renewed order. Use ancient domestic architecture, clothing, and communal life. Treat light as a theological and poetic sign of blessing, not as a modern device.

### Literal/material prompt
Show a protected group experiencing tangible safety and well-being: secure shelter, peaceful daily activity, and a visible contrast with the danger outside. Represent “light” naturally or symbolically without deciding whether it is a physical phenomenon. Do not add protective technology.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate visualization in which a community is sheltered within an unfamiliar luminous environment or protected zone. Keep the source of the light and protection unknown; avoid shields, force fields, domes, or devices unless clearly presented as modern imaginative speculation rather than textual detail.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Show a speculative preserved community maintaining its identity and knowledge after a wider crisis, with several generations living together in safety. Focus on continuity and care rather than genetic distinction. Do not depict laboratories, DNA imagery, or engineered descendants.

### Human / social prompt
Visualize peace as social wholeness: reconciliation, care for vulnerable people, shared food, safe homes, and a community able to live without fear. Let light reinforce the mood of hope without becoming a literal technological beam.

## 1:9 — The Holy Ones accompany the judge

### Ancient-horizon prompt
Create an ancient royal-court and divine-warrior procession: a supreme judge arrives with a vast host of holy attendants, while human wrongdoing is brought into the open. Use ordered ranks, solemnity, and judicial symbolism. Avoid modern military uniforms or later medieval angel conventions.

### Literal/material prompt
Depict a judge arriving with a very large company to conduct a real act of judgment. Show the host as a multitude, not as a precisely countable census. The verse does not describe their bodies, equipment, or transport, so do not invent those details.

### Astronomic / astronautic hypothesis prompt
Create a speculative alternate visualization of a large group of unfamiliar visitors arriving together above or upon the world, with the scene framed as an inspection or judgment. Avoid stereotyped aliens, recognizable spacecraft, weapons, and technical equipment. The image should express the hypothesis of non-human visitors while making no claim that the text proves their identity.

### Human-origin / seeding hypothesis prompt
Depict a speculative migrating or founding population arriving with a leader and establishing a new social order, but emphasize that this is an alternate hypothesis: the verse itself assigns the host a judicial role, not a settlement or reproductive mission. Do not depict genetic engineering or a literal “seed fleet.”

### Human / social prompt
Show wrongdoing exposed before a universal court: ordinary people and powerful figures alike stand accountable, while the judge's attendants witness the proceedings. Emphasize justice, truth, and the end of impunity rather than revenge or spectacle.
