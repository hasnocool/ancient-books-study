# Formatted Interpretation

This directory contains verse-by-verse interpretive studies of ancient texts. Its purpose is to ask what a passage could have meant within the conceptual world of its earliest hearers, while also testing wider literal, cosmological, technological, and human-origin hypotheses without confusing speculation with textual evidence.

## Interpretive stance

Read each verse first as an ancient hearer might have encountered it: through the vocabulary, social order, cosmology, geography, political imagination, ritual life, and available descriptive categories of the period. Do not assume that an ancient writer possessed modern scientific concepts or terminology. Equally, do not assume that every unusual description is merely metaphor.

The studies may explore:
- **Ancient-horizon reading:** what the words, images, and claims could communicate within the text's ancient world.
- **Literal/material reading:** what the passage describes if its claims are taken as concrete events, places, beings, or objects.
- **Astronomic/astronautic hypothesis:** whether descriptions of heavenly beings, descent, travel, vehicles, lights, or celestial order could be read as observations of unfamiliar technology or visitors.
- **Human-origin / seeding hypothesis:** whether a passage could be interpreted as memory or mythologized account of an advanced human lineage, migration, intervention, or deliberate founding/population of humanity.
- **Human/social reading:** what the passage says about human conduct, authority, kinship, fear, justice, memory, and collective life.

Each verse also has five standalone image-generation prompts—one for each lens above. These are alternate visual interpretations, not five claims that all occurred. The ancient-horizon prompt is the historical baseline; astronautic and human-seeding prompts must be explicitly identified as speculative.

These are interpretive lenses, not conclusions. A reading that is imaginable is not thereby historically demonstrated.

## Evidence discipline

Every verse entry must distinguish:
1. **Text:** what the selected edition actually says.
2. **Ancient semantic horizon:** plausible period meanings and limits of vocabulary.
3. **Contextual inference:** what follows from the passage and its literary setting.
4. **Hypothesis:** a possible reading that goes beyond what the text establishes.
5. **Evidence assessment:** what supports, weakens, or could falsify the hypothesis.

Do not retrofit modern words such as *spaceship*, *alien*, *genetics*, *terraforming*, *DNA*, *simulation*, or *advanced civilization* into the ancient source as though they were its own vocabulary. They may appear in a clearly labelled modern hypothesis, followed by the exact ancient description that prompted the comparison.

Do not treat later religious doctrine, modern folklore, or speculative internet claims as evidence for an ancient author's intended meaning. When historical claims are made, cite primary texts and reputable scholarship in the relevant chapter file. Preserve uncertainty and competing readings.

## Status

The Chapter 1 document is an initial interpretive pilot based on the repository's current Chapter 1 transcription. That transcription is not yet verified as a verbatim copy of the 1893 Charles/Dillmann printing. All interpretations therefore remain provisional and must be revisited if the source wording changes after diplomatic collation.

Interpretation is a separate derivative stream. It does not count as transcription, facsimile proofing, textual collation, or independent human approval.
