# Of Asclepius to the King — Meaning Guide

> **Source:** G. R. S. Mead, *Thrice-Greatest Hermes*, Volume II (1906), supplementary fragment.
> **Status:** Provisional explanation based on the formatted transcription; not a new translation or facsimile-verified edition.

## Central question

The fragment explores how something without an ordinary material body can become perceptible through a body. It moves from mirror-reflections to forms and then to a correspondence between the Sensible and Intelligible Worlds.

## Explanation of the dialogue

### Asclepius's opening question

Asclepius asks the King to consider bodiless things in relation to bodies. The mirror example makes the question concrete: a reflection appears in a physical surface but is not itself another material body standing inside the mirror.

**Meaning:** The reflection provides an analogy for presence without ordinary material embodiment. It is an illustration, not necessarily a complete metaphysical definition.

### Forms appearing in bodies

Asclepius next points to forms that appear through bodies, including both ensouled and non-ensouled things.

**Meaning:** The passage distinguishes a form from the material object through which it is manifested. The text's terminology should be retained rather than replaced with a modern theory of perception or information.

### Reciprocal reflection between the two Worlds

Asclepius describes reflections of bodiless things on bodies and of bodies on bodiless things, identifying these with relations between the Sensible and Intelligible Worlds.

**Meaning:** The fragment proposes correspondence across two levels of reality. Its analogy is reciprocal: the relation is not described solely as a one-way descent from an ideal realm into matter.

### Worship of images and the King's response

Asclepius concludes that images should receive worship because their forms come from the Intelligible World. The King then ends the session and schedules a continuation.

**Interpretive caution:** This brief statement should not be generalized into a complete Hermetic doctrine of images, icons, or ritual practice without comparison to other passages and historical scholarship.

## Textual watchlist

- Preserve bracketed “[of it],” “[are they],” “[then,]” and “[reflexions]” pending facsimile collation.
- Check the parenthetical stage directions identifying the King's replies and his rising.
- Confirm punctuation and capitalization in “Sensibile,” “Intelligible,” and “World.”
- The fragment's source placement after Tractate XV does not establish that it belongs to that tractate; keep it separately identified as supplementary.
