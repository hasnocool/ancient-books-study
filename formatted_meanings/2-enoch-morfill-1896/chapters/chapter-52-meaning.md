## Short summary

Enoch sets paired blessings and curses before his children. The blessed praise God, bless His works, work with their own hands, preserve their fathers’ foundations, and establish peace inwardly as well as outwardly. The cursed misuse speech, exploit another’s labour, break inherited enactments, or disturb peace. Enoch says these matters will be disclosed in the great judgment.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter uses repeated antitheses to connect speech, labour, inheritance, and peace with moral accountability. Its distinction between spoken peace and peace in the heart makes inward disposition central.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The text does not define the “foundations” or “enactments” of the fathers in detail. Do not turn these phrases into a specific legal code without further evidence.

## Related passages

The chapter closes the preceding ethical teaching by framing conduct as something disclosed at judgment.
