## Short summary

Enoch sees terrifying guardians at the gates of hell and then travels east to Eden, where rest is prepared for the just. Fiery angels guard Eden, and at the final coming they will bring Adam and the forefathers there. The chapter then gives a sequence of blessings for those who fear God, judge impartially, aid the vulnerable, speak truth, show love, and understand the Lord’s works.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter contrasts punishment with the promised rest of the righteous. Eden is described as both open to the third heaven and shut off from the present world. The ethical sayings translate the vision into conduct: reverence, justice without expectation of reward, care for the naked and hungry, defense of orphans and widows, truthfulness, tenderness, and praise.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The descriptions of the hell guardians and Eden are part of the text’s visionary geography. The phrase “at the last coming” is not further defined here. The chapter should not be harmonized with other accounts of the afterlife or the patriarchs’ final destiny.

## Related passages

The movement from visionary geography to practical moral instruction is significant. The blessings do not merely describe the afterlife; they identify the conduct associated with righteousness in the present life.
