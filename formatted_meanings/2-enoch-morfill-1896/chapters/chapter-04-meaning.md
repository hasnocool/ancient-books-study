## Short summary

The fourth chapter presents the first heaven as an ordered realm in which celestial bodies have appointed rulers and attendants.

### Verse 1: Elders, rulers, and two hundred angels

Enoch is shown the elders and rulers of the orders of the stars. The text then identifies two hundred angels who rule the stars and their heavenly service. The wording presents celestial activity as governed and ministered within an ordered hierarchy.

### Verse 2: Movement around the stars

The angels fly with their wings and circle around the stars as the stars move. The verse gives a vivid image of service and motion, but does not explain the mechanics of the stars or the precise duties of each angel.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Cosmic order:** The stars are not depicted as independent or chaotic; they belong to an administered order.
- **Heavenly service:** Angels are assigned responsibility within the celestial realm.
- **Movement and attendance:** The image of angels circling the stars expresses their continual service.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The text does not name the elders or individual star-rulers.
- “Two hundred” is the number given by the source; its symbolic or administrative significance is not explained.
- The passage should not be converted into a modern account of orbital mechanics.
