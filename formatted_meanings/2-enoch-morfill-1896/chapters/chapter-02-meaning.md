## Short summary

This short chapter records Enoch’s final instruction to his children before the ascent. Its central concern is fidelity to God during his absence.

### Verse 1: Uncertainty about the journey

Enoch openly admits that he does not know where he is going or what will happen to him. The narrative therefore does not portray him as possessing complete foreknowledge of the journey.

### Verse 2: Exclusive worship and obedience

Enoch tells his children to remain faithful to God, keep the divine judgements, and reject the worship of vain gods. The reason given is specific: these gods did not make heaven and earth and are destined to perish, as are their worshippers.

The passage links proper worship to creation. The true God is distinguished from idols by the claim that God is the maker of the cosmos. The text does not provide a detailed account here of the identity or origin of the gods being rejected.

### Verses 3–4: Steadfastness and waiting

Enoch asks God to strengthen his children’s hearts in reverence. He then repeats that they must not search for him until the Lord brings him back. The repetition connects the family instruction to the departure announced in chapter 1.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Faithfulness in absence:** Enoch’s children are to remain loyal even when he is no longer present.
- **Creator versus created idols:** The passage grounds its rejection of vain gods in their inability to claim the creation of heaven and earth.
- **Reverence as steadfastness:** Enoch asks for inward firmness, not merely outward compliance.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The passage does not name particular rival deities or describe their cults.
- “Fear” or reverence for God should not be reduced automatically to panic; the immediate context concerns faithful obedience.
- The chapter does not say how long Enoch will be absent.
