## Short summary

Chapter 9 explains who is intended to inherit the garden shown in the preceding chapter: those whose faithfulness is expressed through endurance, justice, and practical care for others.

### Verse 1: The character of the righteous

The description combines resistance to affliction with moral conduct. The righteous turn away from unrighteousness and accomplish just judgement. Their righteousness is also practical: they feed the hungry, clothe the naked, raise the fallen, and assist oppressed orphans.

The passage therefore does not define righteousness only as inward belief or ritual observance. It includes concrete acts toward vulnerable people.

### Exclusive service and inheritance

Those who walk blamelessly before the Lord and serve him alone receive the garden as an eternal inheritance. The promise links the garden's abundance to a life of fidelity and ethical responsibility.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Endurance:** The righteous remain faithful under attack and affliction.
- **Justice:** Right judgement is part of the description of a righteous life.
- **Care for the vulnerable:** Feeding, clothing, lifting up, and protecting orphans are explicitly named.
- **Exclusive devotion:** The inheritance is associated with serving the Lord alone.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The chapter gives a concise portrait, not a complete catalogue of every virtue.
- It does not explain the mechanics or timing of the eternal inheritance.
- The ethical acts should not be treated as optional decorative details; they are central to the chapter's description.
