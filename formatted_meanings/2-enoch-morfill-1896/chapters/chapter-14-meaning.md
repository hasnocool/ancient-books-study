## Short summary

Chapter 14 completes the account of the sun's gates by describing the western gates, the removal of the sun's crown, and its restoration before dawn.

### Verse 1: Western counterparts

Enoch sees six western gates corresponding to the eastern gates. The sun sets through them according to the stated annual count of 365 days and a quarter. The east-west correspondence gives the solar course a paired structure.

### Verse 2: The crown is carried to the Lord

Four hundred angels take the sun's crown when it passes through the western gates and bring it to the Lord. The text presents this as part of the sun's daily cycle.

### Verse 3: Night and the return of the crown

The sun travels without light for seven complete hours at night. As it nears the east at the eighth hour, the angels return its crown and place it upon it. The image marks the transition from night to renewed daylight.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Paired cosmic gates:** Eastern and western gates structure the sun's daily and annual movement.
- **Angelic ritual service:** The four hundred angels remove and restore the sun's crown.
- **Night and renewal:** The sun's loss and recovery of its crown accompanies the transition from darkness to light.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The chapter does not explain the crown's material or symbolic meaning.
- The seven hours and eighth-hour return are preserved as stated; the passage does not define a modern clock system.
- The annual count is quoted from the source and is not independently recalculated here.
