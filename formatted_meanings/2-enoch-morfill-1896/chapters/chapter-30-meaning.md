## Short summary

Chapter 30 combines a creation sequence with an account of human constitution and moral choice. Its seven substances and seven faculties are separate lists and should not be rearranged into a later system. Adam is both earthly and heavenly in constitution, receives rule and wisdom, and is given a choice between light and darkness. The passage's distinctive wording about Eve and death is retained rather than harmonized with other creation accounts.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Creation and moral order:** The chapter develops its own account of the created world and human conduct.
- **Textual specificity:** Names, numbers, and descriptions are preserved in the form transmitted by this witness.
- **Interpretive restraint:** Apparent tensions are documented rather than silently resolved.
