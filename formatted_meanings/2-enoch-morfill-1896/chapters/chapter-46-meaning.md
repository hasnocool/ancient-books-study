## Short summary

Enoch compares insincere gifts to a prince with flattering speech that conceals hostile intent. In both cases, the hidden heart makes the outward gesture false. A great light sent by God will expose the just and unjust, leaving nothing concealed.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter makes intention central to moral evaluation. Gifts and words cannot disguise disloyalty or evil plans. The image of divine light expresses disclosure: judgment reveals what outward appearances conceal.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The “great light” is not identified more specifically. The chapter does not state whether it is a physical phenomenon, a symbol, or a feature of the judgment scene.

## Related passages

The comparison with an earthly ruler makes the moral principle accessible before applying it to divine judgment. The focus is the alignment of outward conduct and inward purpose.
