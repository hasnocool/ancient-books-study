## Short summary

Chapter 7 introduces a scene of angelic confinement and raises the question of whether Enoch can intercede for the imprisoned beings.

### Verse 1: The second heaven

Enoch is taken to the second heaven and shown prisoners suspended in darkness while awaiting eternal judgement. Their confinement is presented as a current condition with a final judgement still ahead.

### Verses 2–3: The prisoners' identity

The prisoners are described as gloomy and continually weeping. The guides identify them as angels who apostatized from the Lord, disobeyed divine commandments, followed their own will, and transgressed with their prince. The passage gives this explanation but does not name the prince.

### Verses 4–5: A request for prayer

Enoch feels pity, and the angels ask him to pray for them. His answer emphasizes the distance between his mortal condition and their angelic nature. He does not presume authority to intercede and acknowledges uncertainty about his own journey.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Apostasy and judgement:** The angels' confinement follows their rejection of divine command.
- **Compassion:** Enoch responds with pity even toward condemned heavenly beings.
- **Limits of human intercession:** Enoch questions whether a mortal can pray on behalf of angels.
- **Unresolved destiny:** The prisoners await eternal judgement; the passage does not narrate its outcome.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The prince is not named in this chapter.
- The text does not explain the precise nature of the angels' punishment beyond suspension, darkness, and continual weeping.
- Enoch's response is a question, not a universal theological ruling about whether humans may pray for angels.
