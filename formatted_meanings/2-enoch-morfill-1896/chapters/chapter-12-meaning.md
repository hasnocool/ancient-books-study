## Short summary

Chapter 12 introduces two extraordinary kinds of flying creature associated with the sun's chariot.

### Verse 1: Names and appearance

The creatures are called phoenixes and chalkadri. Their bodies combine features of lions and crocodiles; their colour is purple like a rainbow, and their size is stated as nine hundred measures. The description is deliberately marvellous. The source does not explain the unit “measures.”

### Verse 2: Their service to the sun

Each creature has twelve wings, compared with the wings of angels. They attend the sun's chariot and bring heat and dew according to God's command. Their role is therefore defined by service and obedience within the heavenly order.

### Verse 3: The sun's uninterrupted course

The sun makes its revolutions beneath the heavens and beneath the earth, continually giving light through its beams. The verse closes the creature description by returning attention to the sun's ongoing course.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Marvellous beings:** The creatures combine animal features in an extraordinary form.
- **Divinely ordered service:** Their actions occur at God's command.
- **Solar cycle:** Their work is connected with the sun's continuing movement and the provision of heat and dew.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The text does not define “chalkadri” or explain the name's origin.
- “Nine hundred measures” has no unit specified in the passage.
- The creatures should not be identified with zoological species or later symbolic systems without external evidence.
