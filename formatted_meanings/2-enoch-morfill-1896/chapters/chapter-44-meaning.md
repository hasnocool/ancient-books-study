## Short summary

Because God made human beings in the likeness of His countenance, an insult against another person is treated as an insult against the Lord. Enoch condemns unprovoked anger and humiliating spitting, praises those who assist the injured and oppressed, and describes judgment as a process in which measures and weights receive their due measure.

## Key themes and concepts

Human dignity is grounded in the relationship between the human face and the divine countenance. The chapter’s examples make this principle practical: avoid malice and insult, help people under judgment or injury, and raise up the oppressed. The commercial image of weights and standards extends accountability into ordinary economic dealings.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The phrase about measures, standards, and weights is not a technical account of ancient commercial law. The text uses the language of trade to express proportional judgment and recompense.

## Related passages

The chapter joins interpersonal ethics to fair dealing. It treats disrespect toward people and dishonesty in measurement as matters that reach beyond private conduct into divine judgment.
