## Short summary

Enoch compares the differing honour of years with differences among people. He lists possessions, wisdom, understanding, cunning, silence, purity, strength, beauty, youth, mental sharpness, bodily quickness, and perception. Above all these distinctions, he places fear of God.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter acknowledges a broad range of human qualities rather than identifying honour with wealth alone. Its conclusion gives these qualities a hierarchy: reverence for God surpasses all other forms of distinction.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The list does not define how each quality is measured or ranked. The analogy between years and people is not developed into a formal scheme.

## Related passages

The central claim is that social, intellectual, physical, and personal distinctions are secondary to reverence for God.
