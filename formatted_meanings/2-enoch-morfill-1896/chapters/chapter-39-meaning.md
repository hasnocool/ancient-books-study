## Short summary

Enoch tells his children that his message concerns past, present, and future events before judgment. Though they hear a mortal man, he claims to speak the Lord’s words. His descriptions of the Lord’s face, eyes, hand, form, and voice convey terrifying majesty.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter distinguishes the human messenger from the source of the message. Enoch’s repeated insistence that he speaks from the Lord’s lips establishes prophetic authority while acknowledging his mortal appearance. The similes of heated iron, sunlight, thunder, and clouds communicate intensity and awe.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The descriptions are visionary comparisons, not literal measurements or anatomical claims. The chapter does not provide a systematic account of the divine form.

## Related passages

The final comparison with an earthly prince argues from familiar political fear to the greater terror of standing before the Lord of lords. The chapter’s emphasis is the gravity of receiving and transmitting divine speech.
