## Short summary

Chapter 5 continues the tour of the first heaven by showing Enoch the storage places associated with weather.

### Verses 1–2: Heavenly storehouses

Enoch is shown treasuries of snow and ice, guarded by angels. The messengers also show him the treasuries from which clouds emerge and into which they return. The passage represents familiar earthly weather as part of an ordered heavenly system.

The language of treasuries suggests that these phenomena are held and administered rather than appearing outside divine governance. The text does not explain the physical contents or location of these storehouses, nor does it describe the processes by which snow, ice, or clouds form.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Heavenly administration:** Angels guard the treasuries associated with natural phenomena.
- **Ordered creation:** Snow, ice, and clouds are represented as part of a structured cosmos.
- **Visible and invisible order:** Familiar earthly weather is connected to hidden heavenly storehouses.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The passage does not describe the physical contents or location of the treasuries.
- It does not explain weather through modern meteorology.
- The angelic guardians are part of the text’s cosmological presentation; their role should not be silently translated into a modern scientific mechanism.
