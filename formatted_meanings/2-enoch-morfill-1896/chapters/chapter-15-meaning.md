## Short summary

Chapter 15 joins the song of the phoenixes and chalkadri to the explanation of the sun's course and the reckoning of the year.

### Verses 1–2: Praise at the coming of light

The phoenixes and chalkadri sing, and every bird claps its wings in joy at the giver of light. Their song is commanded by the Lord and celebrates the light that comes to the whole world. The passage connects the sun's arrival with a cosmic act of praise.

### Verses 3–4: The sun and the year

Enoch is shown the calculation of the sun's movement. The great gates through which it enters and departs were made by God for the computation of the year. The chapter closes with the statement that the sun is great.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Creation's praise:** Birds and heavenly creatures respond to the coming of light with song and movement.
- **Divine command:** The song takes place at the Lord's command.
- **Timekeeping:** The sun's gates are associated with the calculation of the year.
- **Light as a gift:** The sun's brightness is described as coming to the whole world.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The chapter does not give a new numerical schedule for the sun's course.
- “The giver of light” is the wording used in the song; the guide does not turn it into a separate named being.
- The statement that the sun is great is left as the text's conclusion rather than expanded into an unstated doctrine.
