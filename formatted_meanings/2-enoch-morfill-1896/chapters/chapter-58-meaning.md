## Short summary

Enoch recalls how the Lord brought the animals and birds before Adam, who named them and received authority over them. Yet this authority carries responsibility: the Lord will judge human beings in relation to animals. Animals have their own appointed place and none of the souls God made will perish before the great judgment. An animal may bring a charge against a person who feeds it badly.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter places human dominion alongside accountability for the treatment of animals. The animals are not merely possessions; their welfare matters in divine judgment.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The claims about the destiny and testimony of animal souls belong to this passage and should not be harmonized with other theological systems. Preserve the sequence of creation, naming, dominion, and accountability.

The chapter introduces the animal-protection rules developed in Chapter 59.
