## Short summary

Enoch extends the warning from harm to animals to harm against human beings. Injuring or killing another person, preparing a trap or weapon, and speaking crookedly or maliciously all bring judgment upon the perpetrator. The passage repeatedly describes such acts as leaving no salvation or righteousness for the wrongdoer.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter presents violence and malicious speech as self-destructive acts with consequences in the great judgment.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The repeated absolute formulas should be preserved as the rhetoric of this passage, not softened or expanded into a complete account of repentance elsewhere in the work.

## Related passages

The human-directed prohibitions follow the preceding chapter’s teaching about injury to animals.
