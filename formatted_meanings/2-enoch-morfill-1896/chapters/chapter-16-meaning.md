## Short summary

Chapter 16 presents the moon's movement through twelve gates and gives a calendrical comparison between lunar and solar time.

### Verse 1: Twelve gates

The moon enters and leaves through twelve great gates extending from west to east. The gates mark its customary times and structure the account of its revolutions.

### Verse 2: The sequence of days

The chapter assigns a number of days to each gate: thirty-one days at gates one, two, five, six, eight, nine, and eleven; thirty days at gates three, four, seven, and ten; and twenty-eight days at gate twelve. These values are retained in the order given by the transcription.

### Verses 3–5: Lunar and solar years

The text gives the solar year as 365 days and a quarter and the lunar year as 354 days. It also states that the lunar year has twelve months of twenty-nine days, with eleven days remaining as lunar epacts in the solar year.

There is an arithmetic tension within the wording: twelve months of twenty-nine days total 348 days, not 354. This guide preserves both statements as they occur in the source rather than silently changing either one.

### Verse 6: The quarter day and seasonal adjustment

The quarter day is said to be neglected for three years and completed in the fourth. The passage then describes changes in the seasons and the use of additional and diminishing months to complete the count. Its wording is difficult and is not regularized into a modern calendar system here.

### Verses 7–8: The moon's continuing course

The moon returns through the western gates toward the east, moving through the heavenly circles more swiftly than the winds. Spirits, creatures, and six-winged angels also fly in these circles. The final verse refers to seven months being computed to the moon's circle during a nineteen-year revolution.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Lunar order:** The moon's course is structured by twelve gates and appointed periods.
- **Calendar reckoning:** The chapter compares solar and lunar year lengths and addresses their difference.
- **Cycles and adjustment:** The quarter day, seasonal changes, and nineteen-year revolution appear in the text's account of time.
- **Heavenly movement:** The moon's course is accompanied by spirits, creatures, and angels.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- Twelve months of twenty-nine days total 348 days, while the source also states 354 days; both statements are retained.
- The phrase about seven months in a nineteen-year revolution is not explained further.
- Verse 6's calendrical wording should be checked against the printed page before emendation.
- The chapter's gates are part of its cosmological description, not a modern astronomical model.
