## Short summary

Enoch says that every person’s deeds are recorded and cannot be hidden. He urges patience and meekness, endurance of injury and insult for the Lord’s sake, refusal to take revenge even when one has the power, and generosity toward a brother, an orphan, a widow, and a stranger.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter’s ethics rest on confidence that deeds are known and judgment belongs to God. Non-retaliation is not presented as denial of injustice, but as refusal to become the agent of vengeance. Material generosity is a concrete expression of concern for others.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The final sentence includes a grammatical shift between spending wealth for a brother and extending hands to vulnerable people. Preserve the source’s wording rather than smoothing away its form.
