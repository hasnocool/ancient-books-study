## Short summary

Chapter 6 adds dew to the ordered heavenly storehouses already shown to Enoch.

### Verse 1: Dew and its guardians

The text compares the appearance of dew to anointing oil and says it displays the range of earthly colours. Many angels keep the treasuries, opening and shutting them. The image extends the chapter 5 presentation of snow, ice, and clouds: familiar natural phenomena are represented as held within a heavenly order.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Ordered natural phenomena:** Dew has its own treasury within the narrated cosmos.
- **Angelic guardianship:** Angels are assigned to open and close the storehouses.
- **Sensory description:** The comparison with oil and earthly colours makes the heavenly treasury imaginable through familiar appearances.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The text does not explain how dew is formed or what the treasury physically contains.
- The comparison to anointing oil is descriptive; no ritual meaning is explicitly supplied.
- The phrase about all earthly colours should be retained without imposing a modern optical explanation.
