## Short summary

Chapter 13 describes the eastern gates through which the sun emerges and relates them to the months, hours, and seasons.

### Verse 1: Appointed gates and times

The sun emerges through gates at appointed seasons, in relation to the months of the year and the hours of day and night. The passage presents the solar year as governed by an ordered system.

### Verse 2: Six gates and their measure

Enoch sees six great gates and gives their size as sixty-one stadia and a quarter of a stadium. He emphasizes that he measures them. The measurement belongs to the text's cosmological description; the passage does not provide a modern geographical location for the gates.

### Verses 3–4: Periods of passage

The source assigns forty-two days to the first gates and thirty-five days to several others. In the forward list, it names the first, second, fourth, fifth, and sixth gates, leaving the third unmentioned. The return list names the sixth, fifth, fourth, third, and second gates with periods of thirty-five days. This guide preserves the sequence and omission rather than inserting a conjectural value.

### Verse 5: The seasons

The chapter relates the completion of the year's days to the alternation of four seasons. The gates therefore function as part of the text's account of the annual solar cycle.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Calendrical order:** Solar movement is linked to months, hours, and seasons.
- **Measurement:** Enoch's act of measuring reinforces the ordered character of the vision.
- **Six-gate structure:** The sun's path is described through a sequence of eastern gates and corresponding western movement.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The duration for the third gate is absent from the forward list in the supplied transcription; it is not supplied here.
- The numerical schedule should not be recalculated or harmonized without a separate textual study.
- “Stadia” is the unit used by the translation; the passage does not specify a modern conversion.
