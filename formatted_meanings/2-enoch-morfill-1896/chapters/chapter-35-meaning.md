## Short summary

The chapter imagines Enoch’s writings surviving beyond the lifetime of the family that first receives them. Their preservation depends on guardians who pass them to truthful readers, and those readers in turn transmit them to another generation.

## Key themes and concepts

The writing is presented as a means by which knowledge of God can outlast its first hearers. The promise is conditional in tone: the books are entrusted to people described as true and pleasing to the Lord, rather than to indiscriminate custodians.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The “insatiable” descendants are not defined more precisely. The chapter does not identify the later generations by historical name or give a date for the books’ rediscovery.

## Related passages

The passage links family continuity, textual preservation, and the future reception of Enoch’s teaching. It should not be read as an independently documented account of a specific manuscript’s later history.
