## Short summary

Enoch tells his children to give to the poor according to their means, use rather than bury their silver, assist the honest person in distress, and endure oppressive burdens for the Lord’s sake. He then prescribes praise in the house of the Lord morning, afternoon, and evening, ending with a call for every breathing, visible, and invisible creature to glorify the Creator.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter combines practical generosity with worship. Wealth is to be directed toward those in need, while hardship is met with endurance and hope for judgment. The final lines expand the scope of praise from the human household to all creation.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The expression “honest man” is retained as the source gives it; the chapter does not define the category further. The phrase “violent and grievous yoke” is not explained as a specific historical institution.

## Related passages

The chapter concludes the present instruction with a movement from aid to the poor, through daily worship, to universal praise. It should be read as the close of this unit, not as proof that Enoch’s entire speech or work ends here.
