## Short summary

The chapter says that offerings brought before the Lord are associated with divine response and heavenly treasure, but then insists that God does not need bread, light, animals, or other sacrifices. What God requires is a pure heart.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter distinguishes the symbolic act of giving from the inward disposition that gives it meaning. The offerings are not valuable because God lacks material things; they test the heart of the giver.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The text does not explain the exact ritual form of the lamp or offerings. It does not say that material acts are entirely meaningless; rather, it denies that God needs them and makes purity of heart decisive.

## Related passages

The tension between promised reward for offerings and the statement that God requires a pure heart should be preserved. The chapter’s final emphasis is inward integrity.
