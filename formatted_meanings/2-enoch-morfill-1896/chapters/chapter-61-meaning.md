## Short summary

Enoch urges his children to guard against unrighteousness and treat every living soul with care. He describes many mansions prepared for people: good ones for the good and evil ones for the evil. The blessed mansions offer rest, while the evil ones offer no rest or return. He then teaches that offerings must involve both honest work and a willing heart; labour without the heart, or a murmuring heart without fitting labour, brings no success.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter joins moral conduct, differentiated post-mortem destinations, and the integrity of offerings. Action and inward intention are both required.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The passage does not map the mansions or explain their number. Its two examples concerning work and the heart should remain distinct rather than be collapsed into one formula.

## Related passages

The teaching about the mansions develops the earlier warnings about judgment; the offering instruction leads into Chapters 62–63.
