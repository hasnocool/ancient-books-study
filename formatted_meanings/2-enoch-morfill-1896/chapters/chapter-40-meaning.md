## Short summary

Enoch claims to have recorded the heavens, stars, solar and lunar courses, seasons, time divisions, plants, weather, winds, geography, and the place of judgment. He also records the deeds and sentences of those awaiting judgment.

## Key themes and concepts

The catalogue presents creation as ordered and knowable through divine disclosure. Keys, treasuries, guardians, balances, and measures repeatedly suggest that forces such as wind, rain, thunder, and lightning operate within limits. Enoch’s record joins natural phenomena to moral accountability.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The chapter’s cosmological geography and measurements belong to its visionary framework. They are not to be treated as verified scientific descriptions. The text’s statements about the number and names of stars are Enoch’s claims within the narrative.

## Related passages

The chapter moves from celestial order to earthly life and finally to judgment. Its comprehensive catalogue supports Enoch’s claim to have received extraordinary knowledge, but does not establish how that knowledge was measured or recorded.
