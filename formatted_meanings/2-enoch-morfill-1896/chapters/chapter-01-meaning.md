## Short summary

This chapter introduces Enoch’s extraordinary departure from ordinary human life. He receives a heavenly summons through two radiant messengers. The passage presents the ascent as an act initiated by God, not as an achievement Enoch claims for himself.

### Verses 1–2: Enoch’s age and setting

The opening gives Enoch’s age at the birth of Methusal and the total length of his life. The narrative then locates the experience on the first day of the first month, while Enoch is alone at home and resting. The date may give the episode a formal beginning, but the text does not explain a calendrical symbolism, so one should not impose one.

### Verses 3–7: Grief, vision, and fear

Enoch first experiences grief in a dream without understanding its cause. The two visitors are described through intense light, fire, unusual clothing, and extraordinary bodily features. These details mark them as beings unlike ordinary earthly visitors. Enoch’s fear and obeisance show that the encounter overwhelms him.

### Verses 8–10: The summons

The messengers identify their mission as coming from the everlasting God. Enoch is told he will ascend that day, but also that his household should not search for him until the Lord returns him. The wording establishes both the divine initiative and the temporary uncertainty of Enoch’s absence.

The chapter ends with Enoch telling his sons what has happened. Their names are given in the source as Methusal, Regim, and Gaidal; the bracketed presentation of “and Gaidal” in the transcription should be retained as an editorial feature rather than silently regularized.

## Key themes and concepts

- **Divine initiative:** The messengers are sent by God; Enoch does not initiate the journey.
- **Encounter with the heavenly:** Radiance, fire, wings, and extraordinary stature distinguish the visitors from ordinary humans.
- **Human response:** Enoch reacts with fear and obeisance, while the messengers reassure him.
- **Family and absence:** Enoch informs his sons and leaves instructions concerning his temporary disappearance.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

- The text does not identify the two messengers by name in this passage.
- The dream-grief is reported but not explained.
- The text gives Enoch’s ages and the date, but does not explain their symbolic significance.
- The names and bracketed wording should be checked against the source witness before editorial normalization.
