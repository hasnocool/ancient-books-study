## Short summary

Enoch warns his children not to rely on the idea that their father’s prayer can release them from their own sins. He says that human works are written before creation and cannot be altered by human speech. God sees even thoughts hidden in the heart. Enoch urges his children to heed his instruction now rather than later complain that he failed to warn them.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter emphasizes personal accountability, divine knowledge, and the permanence of the record of deeds. Enoch’s warning makes instruction a present responsibility.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The statement that no one can help a sinner should be reported as Enoch’s warning here, not generalized into a complete doctrine of intercession across all texts.

## Related passages

The chapter develops the preceding chapter’s claim that conduct will be revealed in judgment.
