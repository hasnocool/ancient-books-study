## Short summary

Enoch teaches that wrongdoing against animals also wrongs one’s own soul. He connects the offering of clean animals and birds with the preservation of the offerer’s soul, gives an instruction about binding animals by their four feet when preparing them for food, and condemns killing an animal without a wound or secretly injuring it.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter links ritual offering, food preparation, and humane treatment. Its ethical argument is that violence toward animals rebounds upon the human agent.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The instruction about binding by four feet and its gloss as “an atonement” are unusual; retain them as the source states them rather than silently substituting a familiar rule. The chapter does not explain every practical detail.

## Related passages

This passage applies Chapter 58’s claim that humans are accountable for animal souls.
