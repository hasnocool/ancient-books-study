## Short summary

Enoch rejects swearing by created things and recalls God’s declaration that there is no swearing or injustice in Him, but truth. He recommends simple truthful affirmation—yes or no—and then declares that a place has been prepared for every soul and that each person has an appointed measure of testing in this world.

## Key themes and concepts

The chapter connects truthful speech with divine truthfulness. Its assurance about a prepared place for every soul presents human life as measured and known within the divine order.

## Interpretive limits and open questions

The chapter does not explain the nature or location of the prepared place, nor how the measure of testing is determined. Avoid importing a detailed doctrine of predestination or the afterlife.

## Related passages

The repetition of the assurance is part of the source’s emphasis. The text’s formulation should be preserved without forcing it into a later theological system.
