# Editorial Style Guide

## 1. Editorial modes

Every derivative must declare which mode it uses.

### Faithful formatted transcription (default)

Preserves the source edition's wording and order. Permitted changes are structural and typographic: headings, paragraph breaks, whitespace, consistent quotation marks where safe, and removal of clearly identified scan artifacts. Do not modernize spelling, grammar, vocabulary, or theology.

### Modern-language adaptation

Re-expresses a source in contemporary language. This is a new adaptation, not a transcription. Label it prominently, identify the source passage or section, and keep the adaptation separate from the source wording.

### Study edition

Presents the source text alongside separately labelled explanations, historical context, textual notes, and cross-references. Commentary must never be blended into the source text.

## 2. Document structure

Use this hierarchy unless the source requires another structure:

- `# Work title`
- blockquote subtitle or collection/volume designation
- edition record and source statement
- `## Book / Part / Tractate`
- `### Chapter` when the source has chapters
- `#### Section / Verse` only when an additional level is needed

Keep the source's original numbering. Do not invent chapter or verse numbers. If the source has no sectioning, use paragraph breaks and stable editorial paragraph IDs only when needed.

## 3. Text fidelity

- Preserve the source language, wording, sequence, and meaningful punctuation.
- Preserve translator/editor brackets such as `[...]` and ellipses.
- Never silently repair a disputed reading.
- Do not reconstruct missing text from memory or from another edition without recording the variant and its source.
- Preserve meaningful italics, emphasis, tables, poetry lineation, and lists where the source supports them.
- Keep original units, dates, names, and transliteration unless a separate note explains a variant.

## 4. OCR and scan cleanup

The following may be normalized when unambiguous:

- repeated spaces and broken line wraps;
- page headers, footers, running titles, and isolated printed page numbers;
- words broken across lines when the intended word is certain;
- obvious encoding substitutions that do not alter the text.

The following require a note or review:

- unclear names, dates, numbers, symbols, or foreign-language text;
- missing or duplicated sentences;
- uncertain punctuation that changes meaning;
- damaged, illegible, or absent source passages;
- differences between editions or translations.

Use explicit markers when necessary:

- `[illegible]` — source cannot be read;
- `[text missing]` — a gap is visible or documented;
- `[editorial conjecture: …]` — a proposed reconstruction;
- `[OCR uncertain: …]` — text needs verification.

Never use an uncertainty marker to hide an unverified guess.

## 5. Notes and apparatus

Separate editorial content from the historical text.

Use blockquotes prefixed with **Editorial note:** for short clarifications, or a dedicated `## Editorial notes` section for longer discussion. Attribute notes from the historical edition to their author/editor. Do not copy notes from modern copyrighted editions without permission.

Footnotes should use stable numbering and remain attached to their original callouts. If a note cannot be located confidently, record it in the editorial log instead of guessing.

## 6. Metadata and provenance

Record, when known:

- original title and alternate titles;
- author, compiler, or attributed author;
- translator and editor;
- language and textual tradition;
- edition, publisher, place, and date;
- repository source path and stable source URL;
- archive/facsimile identifier;
- copyright or license basis for the specific text and translation;
- extraction method and known OCR limitations;
- verification method and date.

Clearly distinguish an ancient composition date from the date of a surviving manuscript, translation, or printed edition.

## 7. Quality checks before “Verified”

- Every source section is accounted for.
- Numbering is continuous or all gaps are explained.
- No content was dropped during cleanup.
- Headings match the source edition.
- Page furniture is removed without deleting real content.
- Names, numerals, quotations, and non-English terms have been spot-checked.
- The result has been compared with a scan, facsimile, or independent transcription.
- Rights and attribution are documented.

A formatting pass alone is not textual verification.
