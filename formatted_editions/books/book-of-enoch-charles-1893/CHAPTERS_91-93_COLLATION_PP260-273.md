## 2. Translation witness: repository comparison

The repository's book-05-laws-of-enoch-chapters-91-105.md broadly follows the 1893 printed chapter arrangement. This pass does not replace its wording wholesale.

- **XCI.1–2:** Charles prints Enoch's summons as XCI.1 and Methuselah's response as XCI.2. Preserve this division.
- **XCI.3–10:** Broadly follows the printed translation. Pronouns, conjunctions and punctuation should not be normalized from OCR alone.
- **XCI.11:** The 1893 text brackets this verse as an interpolation; the repository also marks it. Preserve it at its printed position after XCI.10.
- **XCI.12–19:** Printed as the continuation of Chapter XCI. Charles argues that XCI.12–17 belong after XCIII.1–10 in the earlier composition, but the repository should preserve printed numbering.
- **XCII.1–5:** The repository preserves the five-verse chapter. At XCII.3, the 1893 printed reading is “the righteous one will arise from sleep”; manuscript alternatives belong in the apparatus.
- **XCIII.1–10:** The repository preserves the first seven weeks. The trailing “—l” at XCIII.1, “j” at XCIII.4 and “; ,” at XCIII.8 are obvious transcription/OCR debris; verify against page images before editing.
- **XCIII.11–14:** Repository marks all four units as interpolations, consistent with Charles. These begin on printed p.274 and are outside this batch.

**Textual-order conclusion:** Charles reconstructs the earlier sequence as XCII; XCI.1–10; XCIII.1–10; XCI.12–19; XCIV onward. This is a literary reconstruction, not the order printed on pp.265–273. XCI.11 and XCIII.11–14 are separately identified as interpolations.

## 4. Charles's commentary, separately attributed

- **Section V:** Charles regards XCI–CIV as a largely coherent work affected by interpolations and dislocations. He identifies XCI.11, XCIII.11–14 and XCVI.2 as interpolations.
- **Literary sequence:** He argues the original began with XCII, then XCI.1–10, XCIII.1–10, XCI.12–19 and XCIV. This is his reconstruction, not an alternative printed translation.
- **Relation to earlier sections:** Charles argues XCI–CIV differs in authorship and outlook from I–XXXVI and LXXXIII–XC, especially in the duration and setting of the Messianic kingdom, timing of judgment, and resurrection doctrine.
- **Authorship/date:** Charles attributes the section to a Pharisaic author and discusses dates including 95–79 BCE or 70–64 BCE, while preferring 104–95 BCE if C.2 is historical. These are Charles's hypotheses, not established facts.
- **XCI.7–10:** Charles reads renewed wickedness as prompting final judgment; “the righteous one” in XCI.10 is collective, and only the righteous attain resurrection in this section.
- **XCI.11:** Charles considers it out of place after XCI.7–9 and XCI.10, and sees it as a composite interpolation drawing language from XCI.7–8 and XCI.12.
- **XCI.12–13:** The eighth week marks the Messianic kingdom and sword period; the house of the Great King means first the Temple, then Jerusalem.
- **XCI.14–17:** Charles finds XCI.14 difficult; he considers the ninth week a period when true religion spreads and judgment is made known to Gentiles. The tenth week ends with judgment on the Watchers; he notes a new heaven but no explicit new earth.
- **XCII:** Charles regards XCII as the real beginning of the independent work and interprets “the righteous one” collectively.
- **XCIII.1–10:** Charles treats these as the first seven world-weeks, distinct from the final three in XCI.12–17. He rejects fixed equal 700-year periods or a rigid seven-generations-per-week scheme, treating the weeks as variable periods marked by major events.
- **XCIII.3–10 historical mapping:** First week—Enoch's birth; second—wickedness, Flood and Noahic covenant; third—Abraham and his seed; fourth—divine manifestations in Egypt, Sinai law and occupation of Palestine; fifth—Temple; sixth—Israelite apostasy, Elijah, Temple destruction and exile; seventh—the author's generation and publication of Enoch's writings.
- **XCIII.11–14:** Charles considers these verses misplaced and foreign to XCI–CIV, associating them with the Book of Celestial Physics. Their detailed collation belongs to the next batch.
