# 1 Enoch — Chapter 92
## Complete modern-language rendering

**Source basis:** The repository transcription in `formatted_editions/books/book-of-enoch-charles-1893/book-05-laws-of-enoch-chapters-91-105.md`, Chapter 92.

**Editorial method:** This is a verse-by-verse modernization of the complete five-verse chapter. It preserves the source's sequence and claims while updating archaic wording and syntax. It is not a new translation from the Ethiopic, and the repository transcription has not been verified as a verbatim copy of the 1893 Charles/Dillmann printing.

---

### Verse 1

This is the complete teaching of wisdom, written by Enoch the scribe. It deserves the praise of all people and serves as a judgment for the whole earth. I write it for all my children who will live on the earth and for the generations yet to come, who will pursue integrity and peace.

### Verse 2

Do not let the passage of time trouble your spirit. The Holy and Great One has appointed a proper time for everything.

### Verse 3

The righteous person will rise from sleep and walk along the path of righteousness. Every part of that person's journey and conduct will be marked by goodness and grace that endure forever.

### Verse 4

God will show kindness to the righteous person and grant lasting integrity. He will give that person strength, so that they may live in goodness and righteousness and walk forever in the light.

### Verse 5

Sin will disappear into darkness forever. From that day onward, it will never again be seen.

---

## Completeness and editorial notes

- All **five numbered verses** present in the repository's Chapter 92 have been rendered.
- No verse has been intentionally omitted, merged with another verse, or replaced by a chapter summary.
- The chapter's opening description of the writing as Enoch's teaching, its reassurance about appointed times, the rising and conduct of the righteous, the promise of enduring light, and the final disappearance of sin are all retained.
- This rendering follows the repository's chapter and verse divisions. It does not silently import wording or a different verse arrangement from another translation.
- The source text's phrase “the righteous one” is rendered inclusively as “the righteous person” where that improves modern readability without changing the passage's meaning.
