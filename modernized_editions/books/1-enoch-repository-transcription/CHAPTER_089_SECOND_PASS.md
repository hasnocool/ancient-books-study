# Chapter 89 — Targeted Editorial Second Pass

**Date:** 2026-11-25  
**Scope:** Modernized Chapter 89, all 77 numbered units, checked against Chapter LXXXIX in the selected formatted repository transcription.  
**Status:** Assistant-led targeted second pass completed; independent human review remains pending.

## Purpose

The chapter is a long animal allegory with parenthetical identifications supplied by the source transcription. The first modernization had moved several of those identifications into the narrative as explanatory appositions. This pass restores their status as source glosses so readers can distinguish the allegorical wording from the identifications.

## Changes made

- Restored the source-style parenthetical gloss for the vessel: “(Noah)” rather than recasting it as “Noah's ark.”
- Kept “(Jacob),” “(Red Sea),” “(Egyptians),” “(Moses),” “(Jordan),” “(Joshua),” “(Saul),” “(David),” “(Solomon),” and “(2nd Temple)” as parenthetical identifications rather than integrating them into the narrative.
- Preserved the source's “Elyah” spelling in the gloss rather than silently normalizing it to “Elijah.”
- Clarified that the Temple identification in verse 50 is supplied by the source, not spoken as part of the vision.
- Left the verse sequence and all 77 verse bodies in place. No historical witness variant was silently substituted.

## Textual issue retained

Charles's Section IV introduction proposes moving the latter part of 89:48 to follow 89:49. The printed verse text places the clause in 89:48. The modernization continues to preserve the printed verse sequence and records the proposed transposition in the chapter's existing critical-apparatus note.

## Limits

This is an editorial consistency pass against the selected repository transcription, not an independent human review, a fresh translation from Ethiopic, or a complete page-image collation of the historical edition. The selected repository transcription's exact textual lineage remains unresolved. The chapter remains pending independent review, especially for the allegorical identifications and historical sequence.
