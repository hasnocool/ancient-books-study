# 1 Enoch — Chapter 107
## Complete modern-language rendering

**Source basis:** The repository transcription in `formatted_editions/books/book-of-enoch-charles-1893/book-06-deluge-chapters-106-108.md`, Chapter 107.

**Editorial method:** Complete verse-by-verse modernization of all 3 numbered verses in the repository transcription. Archaic wording is updated while the source sequence and substance are retained. This is not a new translation from the Ethiopic, and the repository transcription has not been verified as a verbatim copy of the 1893 Charles/Dillmann printing.



---

### Verse 1

I saw it written in the heavenly records: generation after generation will continue to rebel, until a generation of righteousness arises. Then rebellion will be destroyed, sin will disappear from the earth, and every kind of good will come upon it.

### Verse 2

Now, my son, go and tell your son Lamech that the child who has been born is truly his son. There is no deception in this.

### Verse 3

When Methuselah heard the words of his father Enoch, who had revealed all these mysteries to him, he returned and told Lamech what he had learned. He named the child Noah, because he would cause the earth to rejoice in compensation for all the destruction.

---

## Completeness and editorial notes

- All **3 numbered verses** in the repository's Chapter 107 have been rendered individually.
- No verse has been intentionally omitted or replaced by a chapter summary.
- **1917 page-image comparison:** The 1917 Charles/Oesterley scan, printed p. 153, visibly reads in 107:3 that Noah “will comfort the earth after all the destruction.” The selected repository transcription instead says he will “cause the earth to rejoice in compensation for all the destruction.” This modernization retains the repository wording and does not silently substitute the later witness. Printed p. 153 was visually inspected; this is not a full visual collation of Chapter 107.
- This rendering follows the repository's chapter and verse divisions and preserves its chapter sequence; it does not silently substitute another translation's wording or arrangement.
