# 1 Enoch — Chapter 78
## Complete modern-language rendering

**Source basis:** The repository transcription in `formatted_editions/books/book-of-enoch-charles-1893/book-03-luminaries-chapters-72-82.md`, Chapter 78.

**Editorial method:** Complete verse-by-verse modernization of all 17 numbered verses in the repository transcription. Archaic wording is updated while the source sequence and substance are retained. This is not a new translation from the Ethiopic, and the repository transcription has not been verified as a verbatim copy of the 1893 Charles/Dillmann printing.

**Historical-science note:** Ancient astronomical descriptions and numerical models are preserved as part of the source text and are not presented as current scientific fact.



---

### Verse 1

The sun has the following names: the first is Orjares, and the second is Tomas.

### Verse 2

The moon has four names: Asonja, Ebla, Benase, and Erae.

### Verse 3

These are the two great heavenly lights. Their circumferences are like the circumference of heaven, and they are equal in size.

### Verse 4

In the sun's circumference there is a portion of light from which additions are given to the moon. These measured portions are transferred until the sun's seventh portion has been used up.

### Verse 5

They set and enter the western gates, travel around by the north, and emerge through the eastern gates across the face of heaven.

### Verse 6

When the moon rises, it appears in heaven with one fourteenth of its light. After fourteen days it becomes full.

### Verse 7

Fifteen portions of light are added to it, so that on the fifteenth day its light is full, according to the sign of the year. It reaches fifteen portions, while its phases develop through the addition of fourteenth-parts.

### Verse 8

As the moon wanes, its light decreases each day: on the first day it has fourteen parts; on the second, thirteen; on the third, twelve; on the fourth, eleven; on the fifth, ten; on the sixth, nine; on the seventh, eight; on the eighth, seven; on the ninth, six; on the tenth, five; on the eleventh, four; on the twelfth, three; on the thirteenth, two; on the fourteenth, half of one seventh of its full light; and on the fifteenth, all its remaining light disappears.

### Verse 9

In some months the month has twenty-nine days, and once it has twenty-eight.

### Verse 10

Uriel showed me another rule: the side of the moon on which the sun adds light to it.

### Verse 11

Throughout the period when the moon is gaining light, it grows brighter opposite the sun. By the fourteenth day its light is full in heaven, and when it is completely illuminated, it shines at its fullest.

### Verse 12

On the first day it is called the new moon, because on that day light begins to appear upon it.

### Verse 13

The moon becomes full on the day the sun sets in the west. It rises at night in the east and shines throughout the night until the sun rises opposite it. Then it is seen facing the sun.

### Verse 14

On the side where the moon's light first appeared, it then begins to wane until all its light disappears. The month ends, and its circumference is empty and dark.

### Verse 15

At its appointed times, the moon has three months of thirty days and three months of twenty-nine days. During the first period it completes its waning through the first gate in 177 days.

### Verse 16

During its rising period it appears for three months of thirty days each and for three months of twenty-nine days each.

### Verse 17

At night it appears like a human figure for twenty days at a time, and by day it appears like the heavens, because nothing is visible in it except its light.
