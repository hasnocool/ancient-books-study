# 1 Enoch — Chapter 108
## Complete modern-language rendering

**Source basis:** The repository transcription in `formatted_editions/books/book-of-enoch-charles-1893/book-06-deluge-chapters-106-108.md`, Chapter 108.

**Editorial method:** Complete verse-by-verse modernization of all 15 numbered verses in the repository transcription. Archaic wording is updated while the source sequence and substance are retained. This is not a new translation from the Ethiopic, and the repository transcription has not been verified as a verbatim copy of the 1893 Charles/Dillmann printing.



---

### Verse 1

This is another book written by Enoch for his son Methuselah and for those who will come after him and keep the law in the final days.

### Verse 2

You who have done what is good must wait for those days, until those who practise evil are brought to an end and the power of those who rebel is destroyed.

### Verse 3

Wait until sin has disappeared. The names of the wicked will be erased from the book of life and from the records of the holy ones. Their descendants will be destroyed forever, and their spirits will be put to death. They will cry out and lament in a desolate wilderness, burning in a fire where there is no earth.

### Verse 4

I saw something there that looked like an invisible cloud. Its depth was so great that I could not look upon it. I also saw a bright, burning flame, surrounded by shapes like shining mountains that moved back and forth.

### Verse 5

I asked one of the holy angels who was with me: “What is this shining place? It is not heaven, but a burning fire. I hear cries, weeping, lamentation, and terrible pain.”

### Verse 6

The angel answered: “This is the place where the spirits of sinners are cast: those who blaspheme, practise wickedness, and distort everything God declares through the mouths of the prophets about what is to come.

### Verse 7

“Some of these matters are written and recorded above in heaven, so that the angels may read them and know what will happen to sinners. The records also concern the humble, those who discipline their bodies and are rewarded by God, and those who are shamed by wicked people.

### Verse 8

“These are people who loved God. They loved neither gold nor silver nor any of the world's possessions. They surrendered their bodies to suffering.

### Verse 9

“From the time they came into being, they did not long for earthly food. They regarded their bodies as something temporary, like a passing breath, and lived accordingly. The Lord tested them greatly, but their spirits were found pure, so that they might bless his name.

### Verse 10

“I have recorded all the blessings they received in the books. God has assigned them their reward because they loved heaven more than their earthly lives. Although wicked people trampled them, abused them, insulted them, and brought them shame, they still blessed God.

### Verse 11

“Now I will summon the spirits of the good, those who belong to the generation of light. I will transform those who were born in darkness and who did not seek honour for themselves in the flesh, though their faithfulness deserved it.

### Verse 12

“I will bring out those who have loved my holy name, clothing them in shining light. I will seat each one upon a throne of honour.

### Verse 13

“They will shine forever, without end. God's judgment is righteous, and he will reward the faithful with faithfulness in the dwelling place of uprightness.

### Verse 14

“They will see those who were born in darkness cast into darkness, while the righteous shine in splendour.

### Verse 15

“The sinners will cry aloud when they see the righteous shining. But the sinners themselves will go to the place appointed for them, where their days and seasons have been determined. Amen.”

## Completeness and editorial notes

- All **15 numbered verses** in the repository's Chapter 108 have been rendered individually.
- No verse has been intentionally omitted or replaced by a chapter summary.
- **1917 page-image comparison:** The 1917 Charles/Oesterley scan, printed p. 153, has the daggered reading “†look over† it” in 108:4, marking a corrupt or uncertain expression under the witness's notation. The selected repository transcription reads “I could not look thereon”; this modernization renders that as “I could not look upon it.” The separate authentic 1917 Charles/Oesterley facsimile printed p. 153 was visually inspected, and this edition's different wording is retained rather than silently replaced.
- **Additional 1917 text-layer variants:** In 108:3 the 1917 says “holy books,” while the repository refers to “books of the holy ones.” In 108:9 the 1917 uses “everything” as the passing breath, whereas the repository specifies the bodies. In 108:10 the 1917 describes blessings destined for the righteous, while the repository says blessings they received. In 108:11 the 1917 describes honour not being granted in the flesh in proportion to faithfulness; the repository recasts this as not seeking honour. In 108:14 the 1917 says those born in darkness are led into darkness, while the repository says they are cast into darkness. These are retained as source-specific differences, not silently harmonized.
- Printed p. 153 was also visually inspected: it contains the end of Chapter 107 and the opening of the Chapter 108 appendix, verses 1–7. The end of Chapter 108, verses 8–15, appears on p. 154. These two checked pages verify the closing transition and final portion of Chapter 108. Printed p. 152 remains an uninspected page elsewhere in the Chapters 105–108 visual sequence.
- This rendering follows the repository's chapter and verse divisions and preserves its chapter sequence; it does not silently substitute another translation's wording or arrangement.
