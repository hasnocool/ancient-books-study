# 1 Enoch — Chapter 89
## Complete modern-language rendering

**Source basis:** The repository transcription in `formatted_editions/books/book-of-enoch-charles-1893/book-04-visions-chapters-83-90.md`, Chapter 89.

**Editorial method:** Complete verse-by-verse modernization of all 77 numbered verses in the repository transcription. Archaic wording is updated while the source sequence and substance are retained. This is not a new translation from the Ethiopic, and the repository transcription has not been verified as a verbatim copy of the 1893 Charles/Dillmann printing.

---

### Verse 1

One of the four beings went to the white bull and taught him a secret while he trembled. He had been born a bull but became a human being. He built a great vessel—Noah's ark—and lived in it with three other bulls, and they were sheltered inside.

### Verse 2

I looked up toward heaven again and saw a high roof with seven streams of water upon it. Those streams poured a great quantity of water into an enclosure.

### Verse 3

Then I saw springs open in the earth inside that great enclosure. The water swelled and rose until it covered the enclosure and hid it from view.

### Verse 4

Water, darkness, and mist increased. The flood rose higher than the enclosure, overflowed it, and spread across the earth.

### Verse 5

All the cattle in the enclosure gathered together. I watched as they sank, were swallowed, and perished in the flood.

### Verse 6

But the vessel floated on the water. All the oxen, elephants, camels, donkeys, and other animals sank with the rest. I could no longer see them; they could not escape, and they perished in the depths.

### Verse 7

I watched until the streams of water were withdrawn from the high roof. The openings in the earth were filled in, and other abysses were opened.

### Verse 8

The water began to drain into those openings until the earth became visible. The vessel came to rest on the ground, the darkness withdrew, and light appeared.

### Verse 9

The white bull who had become a man came out of the vessel with the three bulls who had been with him. One was white like him, one was red like blood, and one was black. The white bull then departed from them.

### Verse 10

They began producing wild animals and birds, and many different kinds appeared: lions, tigers, dogs, wolves, hyenas, wild boars, foxes, squirrels, pigs, falcons, vultures, kites, eagles, and ravens. Among them a white bull was born.

### Verse 11

The animals began biting one another. The white bull born among them fathered a wild donkey and another white bull, and the wild donkey's descendants multiplied.

### Verse 12

The bull born from him fathered a black wild boar and a white sheep. The wild boar produced many boars, while the sheep—Jacob—fathered twelve sheep.

### Verse 13

When the twelve sheep had grown, they gave one of their number to the donkeys. The donkeys in turn handed that sheep over to the wolves, and it grew up among the wolves.

### Verse 14

The Lord brought the other eleven sheep to live and graze with it among the wolves. They multiplied and became many flocks.

### Verse 15

The wolves became afraid of the sheep and oppressed them. They killed their young and threw them into a river full of water. The sheep cried out over their lost young and complained to their Lord.

### Verse 16

One sheep that had been saved from the wolves fled and escaped to the wild donkeys. I saw the sheep mourning, crying, and pleading with all their strength until their Lord descended from his high dwelling in response to their cries and came to shepherd them.

### Verse 17

He called the sheep that had escaped the wolves and spoke with it about the wolves, instructing it to warn them not to harm the sheep.

### Verse 18

The sheep went to the wolves as the Lord had commanded. Another sheep met it and went with it. Together they entered the gathering of the wolves and warned them never again to attack the sheep.

### Verse 19

I saw how the wolves continued to oppress the sheep with all their strength. The sheep cried out loudly.

### Verse 20

Their Lord came to the sheep and began striking the wolves. The wolves began to lament, while the sheep became calm and stopped crying out.

### Verse 21

I watched the sheep leave the wolves behind. The wolves' eyes had been blinded, but they pursued the sheep with all their strength.

### Verse 22

The Lord of the sheep went with them as their leader, and all his sheep followed. His face was dazzling, glorious, and terrifying to behold.

### Verse 23

The wolves pursued the sheep until they reached a sea of water, the Red Sea.

### Verse 24

The sea divided, and the water stood like walls on either side of the sheep. Their Lord, who led them, placed himself between the sheep and the wolves.

### Verse 25

The wolves could not see the sheep as they entered the middle of the sea. The wolves pursued them and rushed into the sea after them.

### Verse 26

When the wolves saw the Lord of the sheep, they turned and tried to flee from him. But the sea came together again and returned to its natural state. The water surged upward and covered the wolves, the Egyptians.

### Verse 27

I watched until all the wolves that had pursued the sheep perished and drowned.

### Verse 28

The sheep escaped from the water and entered a wilderness where there was neither water nor grass. They began to open their eyes and see. I saw the Lord of the sheep shepherding them and providing water and grass, while the sheep that led them continued to guide them.

### Verse 29

That sheep climbed to the top of a high rock, and the Lord of the sheep sent it to the others.

### Verse 30

After this I saw the Lord of the sheep standing before them. His appearance was great, terrifying, and majestic. All the sheep saw him and were afraid.

### Verse 31

They feared and trembled before him. They cried to the sheep that was among them and said: “We cannot endure the presence of our Lord or look upon him.”

### Verse 32

The sheep that led them climbed to the top of the rock again. But the sheep began to lose their sight and wander away from the path he had shown them. The leading sheep did not join their rebellion.

### Verse 33

The Lord of the sheep became extremely angry with them. The leading sheep learned of this, came down from the rock, and found most of the flock blinded and turned away.

### Verse 34

When the sheep saw it, they were afraid and trembled before it. They wanted to return to their folds.

### Verse 35

The leading sheep took other sheep with it and went to those who had strayed. It began to strike down the rebels. The sheep feared its presence, and it brought the straying sheep back so that they returned to their folds.

### Verse 36

I watched until that sheep became a man and built a house for the Lord of the sheep. He placed all the sheep inside that house.

### Verse 37

I watched until the sheep that had met the leader fell asleep—this was Moses. I saw all the great sheep perish, and smaller sheep arise in their place. They came to a pasture and approached a stream of water, the Jordan.

### Verse 38

Then the sheep that had led them and become a man—Joshua—left them and fell asleep. All the sheep searched for him and mourned loudly.

### Verse 39

I watched until they stopped mourning for that sheep and crossed the stream. Other sheep continually arose as leaders in place of those who had led them and then fallen asleep.

### Verse 40

I watched until the sheep came to a good, pleasant, and glorious land. They were satisfied there, and the house stood among them in that pleasant land.

### Verse 41

At times their eyes were opened, and at other times they were blinded. Then another sheep arose, led them all, brought them back, and their eyes were opened again.

### Verse 42

The dogs, foxes, and wild boars began devouring the sheep. Then the Lord of the sheep raised up another sheep from among them—a ram—to lead them.

### Verse 43

The ram began butting the dogs, foxes, and wild boars on every side until he had destroyed them all.

### Verse 44

The sheep's eyes were opened, and it saw that the ram among them had forgotten his dignity. He began butting the sheep, trampling them, and behaving disgracefully.

### Verse 45

The Lord of the sheep sent a lamb to another lamb and raised that lamb to become a ram and leader of the flock in place of the ram who had forgotten his dignity.

### Verse 46

The new ram went to the other ram and spoke with him privately. He raised him to become a ram and appointed him prince and leader of the sheep. During all this time, however, the dogs continued to oppress the flock.

### Verse 47

The first ram, Saul, pursued the second ram, David. The second ram rose up and fled from him. I watched until the dogs brought down the first ram.

### Verse 48

The second ram arose and led the sheep. He fathered many sheep and then fell asleep. A small sheep took his place and became a ram, prince, and leader of the flock—Solomon.

### Verse 49

The sheep grew and multiplied. All the dogs, foxes, and wild boars feared them and fled. The ram butted and killed the wild animals, which no longer had power among the sheep or stole anything from them.

### Verse 50

The house became large and broad, and a great, lofty tower was built for the sheep. The tower was built above the house of the Lord of the sheep. The house itself was low, but the tower rose high. The Lord of the sheep stood upon the tower, and a full table was placed before him. This represents the Temple.

### Verse 51

Again I saw the sheep stray and wander along many paths. They abandoned their house. The Lord of the sheep called some of them and sent them back to the flock, but the sheep began killing them.

### Verse 52

One of them was spared and not killed. It escaped—this was Elijah—and cried out loudly against the sheep. They wanted to kill it, but the Lord of the sheep rescued it, brought it up to me, and caused it to dwell with me.

### Verse 53

The Lord sent many other sheep to the flock to testify against them and mourn over them.

### Verse 54

After this I saw that when the sheep abandoned the Lord's house and tower, they fell away completely and their eyes were blinded. The Lord of the sheep carried out great slaughter among their separate flocks, until the sheep themselves invited this slaughter and betrayed his dwelling.

### Verse 55

He handed them over to lions, tigers, wolves, hyenas, foxes, and every kind of wild animal. Those animals began tearing the sheep apart.

### Verse 56

I saw that he abandoned their house and tower and handed them all over to the lions and the other wild animals, to be torn apart and devoured.

### Verse 57

I began to cry out with all my strength and appeal to the Lord of the sheep. I pleaded with him because all the wild animals were devouring the sheep.

### Verse 58

But he remained unmoved. He watched and rejoiced as they were devoured, swallowed, and robbed. He left them in the power of all the wild animals.

### Verse 59

He called seventy shepherds and assigned them to pasture the sheep. He said to the shepherds and their companions: “Each of you must shepherd the sheep from now on and do everything I command. I will hand them over to you in numbered groups and tell you which ones must be destroyed. Those are the ones you must destroy.”

### Verse 60

He handed the sheep over to them.

### Verse 61

He called another being and said: “Watch carefully and record everything the shepherds do to these sheep, because they will destroy more of them than I have commanded.”

### Verse 62

“Record every excess and every act of destruction carried out by the shepherds. Count how many they destroy according to my command and how many they destroy on their own initiative. Record the destruction caused by each individual shepherd.

### Verse 63

“Report to me by number how many they destroy and how many they hand over for destruction. This will stand as testimony against them. I want to know every deed of the shepherds, so that when I entrust the sheep to them I can see whether they obey the command I gave.

### Verse 64

“They must not know about this, and you must not tell or warn them. Simply record every act of destruction committed by each shepherd and present the whole account to me.”

### Verse 65

I watched as the shepherds tended the sheep during their appointed periods. They began killing and destroying more sheep than they had been commanded to destroy, handing them over to the lions.

### Verse 66

The lions and tigers ate and devoured most of the sheep, and the wild boars joined them. They burned the tower and demolished the house.

### Verse 67

I became deeply sorrowful over the tower because the house of the sheep had been destroyed. Afterward I could no longer tell whether the sheep entered that house.

### Verse 68

The shepherds and their companions handed the sheep over to every kind of wild animal to be devoured. Each shepherd received a specific number of sheep for his appointed period, and another being recorded in a book how many each shepherd destroyed.

### Verse 69

Each shepherd killed and destroyed many more sheep than had been assigned. I began to weep and mourn for the flock.

### Verse 70

In the vision I saw the scribe recording every sheep destroyed by the shepherds, day after day. He carried the complete book to the Lord of the sheep, laid it before him, and showed him everything they had done: all they had taken away and all they had handed over for destruction.

### Verse 71

The book was read before the Lord of the sheep. He took it from the scribe, read it, sealed it, and put it away.

### Verse 72

Immediately I saw the shepherds tending the flock for twelve periods. Then three sheep turned back, came, and entered the ruined place. They began rebuilding the remains of the house. The wild boars tried to stop them but could not.

### Verse 73

They began rebuilding as before—the Second Temple—and raised the tower again, calling it the high tower. They placed a table before the tower once more, but all the bread upon it was polluted and impure.

### Verse 74

The eyes of these sheep were blinded so that they could not see, and the eyes of their shepherds were blinded too. Many sheep were handed over to the shepherds for destruction. The shepherds trampled them underfoot and devoured them.

### Verse 75

The Lord of the sheep remained unmoved until all the sheep had been scattered across the field and mixed with the other animals. They were not rescued from the power of the beasts.

### Verse 76

The one who wrote the book brought it up, showed it, and read it before the Lord of the sheep. He pleaded on behalf of the flock and presented all the deeds of the shepherds as testimony against them.

### Verse 77

The Lord took the book, placed it beside himself, and departed.
